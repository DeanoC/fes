# i386 exception delivery

Dated record, 2026-10-27. Host simulation and routed diagnostics on Powerboat.
No package, RBF or kit.

After [the microcode slice](2026-10-26-i386-ucode.md), the CPU delivers its
real-mode faults: #UD, #GP(0), #SS(0) and #NM. A fault taken at WB enters
INT's microcode routine with its vector, which pushes FLAGS, CS and the
faulting instruction's IP and jumps through the vector table.

Until now a faulting run stopped at the fault, and the lockstep compared only
its vector. With delivery every faulting corpus state runs on into its
handler, so its final state, the pushed frame included, is compared for the
first time. That found one mismatch, a PUSHA or POPA whose stack wraps, and
this slice makes the two transfer as the 386EX does.

Run data: [`2026-10-27-i386-exceptions.jsonl`](2026-10-27-i386-exceptions.jsonl).
- The before figures are the microcode slice's `54ff8b2149` (its record's
  rows).
- Routes and the full lockstep runs are of `a18ad37ac5`.
- A Quartus row's `fingerprint` is the first 12 hex digits of the SHA-256 of
  its `sources` map, as JSON with sorted keys.

## Delivery

**Entry.** A uop that reaches WB with a fault other than
`FAULT_UNIMPLEMENTED` is delivered (`exc_take`).
- WB flushes everything younger and restarts the front end and DC. The front
  end refetches from the faulting instruction, or the one before, which the
  delivery's far jump later discards.
- The cycle after (`exc_inject`), DC's skid takes the exception's entry,
  `dc_exc`: a NOP at length 0 that starts the ROM's `vector` routine with the
  fault's vector. That is the word INTO enters, renamed from `into`; it reads
  the vector's offset and falls into INT's routine.
- EX's running flags need nothing: a faulting uop changes nothing in EX.

**The frame.** D's EIP is the faulting instruction's, cut to 16 bits.
- At length 0 the routine's push of the next IP pushes that IP, the
  instruction's first byte, prefixes included.
- An instruction that ends at FFFFh leaves EIP 10000h, and the next fetch
  faults. The cut pushes IP 0, as the 386EX does (94 corpus states), and D's
  CS-limit check passes the entry.

**A fault in a delivery stops the CPU** as unimplemented, vector 8, since
double fault and shutdown are not built (`exc_active` holds from the fault
to the delivery's last uop).
- In real mode it is a stack at SP 1, 3 or 5, whose frame straddles the 64 KiB
  wrap however the delivery is retried.
- The oracle faults again there and shuts down.

The delivery's last uop retires with `trace_exception`, which replaces
`trace_fault`.

## PUSHA and POPA

The 386EX moves a PUSHA's or POPA's slots one at a time, from the lowest
address up. A slot that crosses the limit raises #SS(0), and the slots below
it have moved; ESP has not. The corpus's 11 states that wrap the stack
misaligned show it (divergence `stack-wrap-partial-transfer`): PUSHAD has
stored EDI and ESI, POPA has loaded DI, SI and BP.

The microcode slice's probe faulted first and moved nothing, which follows
neither the 386EX nor Bochs. Its checks compared only those states' vector.

| Instruction | Uops now | Before |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PUSHA, PUSHAD | EDI's store at slot -8; then ESI, EBP, ESP (unmoved, the original), EBX, EDX, ECX; EAX's store at -1 steps ESP down past the eight | the probe; ESP into T1; eight pushes |
| POPA, POPAD | DI's load from slot 0; then SI, BP, ESP's slot into T1, BX, DX, CX; AX's load from 7 steps ESP up; POPAD's ESP from T1 on a 16-bit stack | the probe; eight pops; the same last uop |

- **Slots.** A `STK_PEEK` uop's slot is signed, -8 to 7, as a shift of
  operand-sized slots from ESP. One that also writes ESP (`sp_step` in the
  ROM) steps it past eight slots, down from a slot below ESP and up from one
  above.
- **Limits.** Each slot takes the ordinary limit check, so PUSHA and POPA
  now run under any SS limit. Only INT keeps a probe, of its three words, and
  stays unimplemented under a limit other than FFFFh.
- **Deleted:** `PROBE_PUSHA`, `PROBE_POPA`, `b_t1` and the ROM's `b = "t1"`.
  The ROM is 25 words of 63 bits.

## Harness

- **A delivery is a retirement.** The oracle's exception and the RTL's
  delivery must match in vector, and the delivery's state and writes are
  compared as any retirement's.
- **Corpus states** that fault run on into their handler to its HLT, as INT's
  already did, and their final state is compared.
- **Random programs** stop after a delivery: the vector table is zero, so the
  handler would run zeroed memory.
- **A fault in a delivery.** When the oracle faults twice or shuts down, the
  RTL must stop as unimplemented with vector 8, with nothing of the
  instruction retired and nothing of the delivery written.
- **Directed faults.** The three out-of-limit CALL, RET and JMP cases now
  run to a HLT handler, check the pushed CS:IP and ESP, and check that
  fetch, issue and data reads stay quiet after the HLT. Two new cases fault
  in their delivery: INT 20h at SP 1, and the CALL case at SP 3.
- **Two divergences become predicates,** so random programs follow them:
  - `stack-wrap-partial-transfer`, on `slot-straddles-wrap`: PUSHA's or
    POPA's slots wrap the 16-bit stack misaligned.
  - `interrupt-vector-before-push`, on `frame-over-vector`: the frame of the
    exception or interrupt the instruction takes overlaps the vector's
    entry. Bochs pushes first, then reads the overwritten entry. Six random
    programs met this before the predicate.
  - Each selects exactly the corpus states it listed. The vectors runner
    still finds every oracle-versus-corpus difference covered.

## What the corpus now checks

- Every faulting state's final state: 116,331 states in an exception's
  handler, among them RETF's 480 #SS and #GP states, IRET's 146 and the
  pointer loads'. All pass.
- The far slice's rule that a far call writes nothing when its second push
  would cross the stack's limit is still Bochs's and the manual's: the corpus
  has one far call that faults on the stack (an FF /3 #SS).

## Result

| | Before (`54ff8b2149`) | After (`a18ad37ac5`) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| nextpnr `bd7a1c0d` (HIP, gpu-run) | 67.83, 70.97, 72.49, 70.43 (DC, DC, D, DC) | 70.32, 68.82, 71.14, 59.96 (DC ×4) |
| Quartus 17.0.2 (oracle) | 102.98, 94.73, 98.69, 103.27 (Q ×4) | 96.20, 99.64, 99.54, 101.56 (Q ×4) |

**nextpnr averages 67.6 MHz against 70.4.** Every seed is limited by D's
issue into `dc`, the loop the microcode record named.
- The loop is 9-10 cells with no carry, and routing is 10.6-13.4 ns of it:
  `issue` enables nearly all of `dc`.
- Seed 4's 60.0 MHz is that loop with 13.4 ns of routing; the other seeds
  route it in 10.6-11.3 ns.
- Without seed 4 the average is 70.1. The first build below was low on every
  seed, so delivery costs this loop some MHz, not only placement.
- **The loop is now the CPU's limit on nextpnr**, ahead of Q, and the next
  thing to cut: the hold terms and `issue`'s fanout to `dc`.

**The first build**, `884417f51e`, routed 70.32, 62.63, 67.82 and 65.45 on
nextpnr (all the same loop) and 103.59, 101.30, 99.86 and 104.71 on Quartus.
- Its Yosys run showed two costs, both of selects on `exc_take`, which goes
  through `freeze` and so the data port's valid pin.
  - The redirect's address and D's EIP chose by it, which put F1's fetch
    adder behind it: F1 went from 5 LUTs to 8 and a 28-cell carry chain.
  - A restore of EX's running flags on a fault cost EX a level.
- The final commit selects the address on WB's fault field, a register,
  since a taken branch has none. It drops the restore, which a faulting uop
  never needed: it changes nothing in EX, and its mutation found nothing.
- F1 is back to 7 LUTs and Q to 9.

**Quartus averages 99.2 MHz against 99.9,** Q-limited on every seed, on the
front end's byte select, which this slice does not change.

**Yosys's depth:** D 10, DC 11, EX 11, M1 7, M2 8, WB 6, against 9, 11, 10,
7, 7, 6. D's deepest path ends at `t_busy`, EX's at its WB forward and M2's at
`m2_dr`; none runs through anything this slice changes. Yosys moved the
front end's L2, unchanged, from 9 to 10 between these runs too.

## Cycle costs

Without latency, measured on corpus states as cycles from start to HLT,
against a NOP's 17. A delivered exception's run ends at its handler's HLT.

| Instruction | Extra cycles | Before |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A fault delivered (#UD from LOCK MOV, #SS from RETF, #GP from LES) | 32, 32, 33 | stopped |
| INT n, INT3 | 22 | 22 |
| PUSHA | 14 | 18 |
| POPA | 17 | 19 |
| IRET | 20 | 20 |

- A delivery is the fault's trip to WB, the flush and the skid's load, then
  eight uops (the NOP, the vector's offset, INT's six) with the ROM's wait
  for each word, and the far jump's redirect.
- PUSHA and POPA lose their probe and, for PUSHA, the copy of ESP into T1.

## What the design doc should say

This is reported for the doc's owner to write; the doc is unchanged here.

- **Faults** are taken at WB: flush, then INT's routine with the fault's
  vector, entered through a NOP at length 0 so the routine pushes the
  faulting instruction's IP. About 32 cycles to the handler.
- **Double fault and shutdown** are not built: a fault in a delivery stops
  the CPU. The design's SHUTDOWN port and the machine's reset on it are the
  next step there.
- **PUSHA and POPA** move their slots from the lowest address up, each
  checked on its own, as the 386EX does; only INT probes.
- **Clock.** D's issue into `dc` limits every nextpnr seed now. It is a
  routing-dominated loop, `issue` enabling nearly all of `dc`, and each slice
  that touches issue or redirect pays there; it wants its own cut.
- **Next:** the remaining small forms, then MUL and DIV, whose #DE this
  delivers; then strings, I/O and external interrupts.
