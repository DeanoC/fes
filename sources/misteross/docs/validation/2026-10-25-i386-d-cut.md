# i386 D cut: decode in DC, a cycle ahead of D

Dated record, 2026-10-25. Host simulation and routed diagnostics on Powerboat.
No package, RBF or kit.

After the [EX cut](2026-10-25-i386-ex-cut.md), D limited the whole CPU on
nextpnr. This cut moves the decode into a stage of its own, DC, so that D's
issue logic starts from registers. Both flows were re-routed, seeds 1-4, as
in the EX cut:
- nextpnr `bd7a1c0d` through `gpu-run`, HIP, with `NEXTPNR_REQUIRE_GPU=1` and
  an 80 MHz target;
- Quartus 17.0.2 through `scripts/quartus_fes_i386.py --dsp-in-logic` at
  100 MHz.

Run data: [`2026-10-25-i386-d-cut.jsonl`](2026-10-25-i386-d-cut.jsonl). The
before figures are the EX cut's after rows (`7b771a8444`). Their source hashes
match this branch's base exactly, so they were not re-routed.
- A nextpnr row's `fingerprint` is the benchmark's source fingerprint.
- A Quartus row's `fingerprint` is the first 12 hex digits of the SHA-256 of
  its `sources` map, as JSON with sorted keys.

The RTL still carries the DeanoC/yosys#59 workaround in qalign, until
DeanoC/yosys#60 merges into the lock.

| nextpnr Fmax | 62.92, 64.80, 62.91, 62.60 | 69.63, 70.05, 68.62, 74.64 |

| Quartus limit | Q, D, EX, D | Q ×4 |

- **D stops limiting on both flows.**
  - nextpnr: D rises from 62.6-64.8 to 76.9-80.8 MHz, and DC sits at
    77.4-90.7. EX limits at 68.6-74.6, with Q next at 72.8-77.5.
  - Quartus: D rises from 96.3-105.0 to 112.8-115.9 MHz, and DC sits at
    107.2-116.4. The whole CPU closes at 100.4-103.3, limited by Q, with EX
    0.9-1.4 MHz behind it.
- **The cost is one cycle on every redirect.** A taken branch now retires 11
  cycles before the next instruction instead of 10. Nothing else changes in
  timing.

## The path

On nextpnr the EX cut's D paths ran (9-11 cells, 65-72% routing):
1. the skid register (`skid_valid` or `skid`);
2. the skid mux (`cur`);
3. the decode;
4. the issue hazards: the LEA address stall (`dfwd`), the selector hazard and
   the flag hold;
5. `issue`;
6. issue's fanout into `ag_s`, `skid_valid`, `d_stop` and the forwarding
   tables.

A first try, recorded here because it failed, took the hazard terms (`lea`,
`reads_seg`, `seg_index`) from the raw opcode and ModRM bytes, as the EX cut
did for `consumes`. On the same netlist, Yosys's depth to D's registers
stayed at 11 LUT levels and `issue` dropped only from 11 to 10. Its deepest
path went through amode and the forwarding table to `lea_stall`. The fault
and `stop` decode, through `dst.valid` into the forwarding tables, was 10
levels from `cur` on its own. With Q's output register as D's input, no
raw-byte term removes that depth.

## The cut

- **DC decodes Q's output register.** `fes_i386_decode` and `fes_i386_amode`
  run on `q` and fill a `decoded_t` record: the uop, the length, the LEA and
  selector terms, `consumes`, `stop`, and the address registers and scale.
- **A two-entry decoded buffer replaces the skid.**
  - `dc` holds the instruction D issues.
  - `dc_skid` takes Q's instruction when `dc` cannot.
  - Q's `d_ready` is the registered `!dc_skid_valid`, as it was `!skid_valid`,
    so D's stall still stays out of Q's loop.
  - `dc` loads when it is empty or D issues, from `dc_skid` if that holds an
    instruction, else from DC's decode. That select comes from a register.
- **D starts from registers.** Its hazards, its forwarding lookups by register
  number, the EIP incrementer and `issue` all read `dc`.
- **wrap16 moves into D.** It is the 16-bit next-EIP wrap that decodes as
  unimplemented, and only D knows the instruction's EIP.
  - D raises it as `FAULT_UNIMPLEMENTED` when the decode found no other fault,
    and sets `stop`.
  - The uop keeps its destination. That is harmless: WB writes nothing for a
    fault, and D issues nothing after the uop (`d_stop`).
- **No stage behind D holds,** so D's forwarding tables need no new check. DC
  sits ahead of D.

Yosys's depth before placement:

| Stage | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| D | 11 | 9 |
| DC | - | 9 |
| EX | 12 | 11 |

D's deepest path now starts at the forwarding table (`dfwd.in_ag`) and runs
through the LEA stall to `issue` and `dc_load`. Q's and EX's moves are ABC
remapping unrelated logic, as in the EX cut. Routed, Q did not move beyond the
seed spread on either flow.

Size: 6,614 flip-flops against 6,415 (DC's buffer), and 10,307 ALUTs against
10,244. Quartus fits in 6,390-6,429 ALMs against 6,372-6,406.

## Stage labels

The `cpu` spike gains a DC label for `dc`, `dc_skid` and their valid bits. It
sits ahead of D, since `dfwd` and `dops` take DC's fields as ports.

Q's output register now feeds only DC, and synthesis can leave it named only
after DC's inputs:
- `decode.q` in Yosys;
- `decode.by` in Quartus;
- `dc_q`, or a Quartus anonymous register whose alias is `dc_q`.

Q's pattern takes all three. Before that change, 57 of Q's registers fell to
the envelope in Yosys. In Quartus, Q's worst path showed as the envelope's.
After it, the label counts match the base's exactly:
- envelope 1,493 and Q 81;
- D's 517 registers split into D 454 and DC 254, the buffer's two entries
  included.

The Quartus envelope is back at 124.3-132.6 MHz, against 126.9-132.0 before.

## Next limits

**nextpnr: EX, 68.6-74.6 MHz.**
- Every seed's worst path runs from EX's registered controls (`ex.exc`)
  through the result select and the shift CF/OF (`def_co`, `sh_of`) into
  `wb_s`.
- The paths carry 9-11 cells, 64-67% of it routing.
- Q follows at 72.8-77.5, then D at 76.9-80.8.

**Quartus: Q, 100.4-103.3 MHz.**
- The 32:1 select from `align.start` into Q's output register, at 7-8
  levels.
- EX follows within 1.4 MHz.

## What the design doc should say

This is reported for the doc's owner to write; the doc is unchanged here.

- **Stages.** D splits into DC (decode and the addressing decode) and D (the
  register reads, forwarding, hazards and issue). The pipeline is twelve
  stages as built.
- **Latency table.**
  - A mispredict resolved at EX costs 1 cycle more.
  - So would one resolved at AG, once AG resolves branches.
  - The first instruction after a redirect issues a cycle later.
- **Clock.**
  - Quartus closes the whole CPU at 100.4-103.3 MHz.
  - nextpnr closes it at 68.6-74.6, EX-limited, behind the place-and-route
    gap DeanoC/nextpnr#300 records.
- **The trade.** With a mispredict rate like the
  [estimate](../i386-cpu-design.md#performance-estimate)'s, the cycle costs
  about 1% of throughput against about 10% of clock on nextpnr.
