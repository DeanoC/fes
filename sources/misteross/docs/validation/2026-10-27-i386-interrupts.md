# i386 interrupts: INTR, NMI, the shadow and HLT

Dated record, 2026-10-27. Host simulation and routed diagnostics on Powerboat.
No package, RBF or kit.

This slice builds INTR and NMI on the machine interface's interrupt lines, as
the design's [commit, exceptions and interrupts](../i386-cpu-design.md#commit-exceptions-and-interrupts)
section asks: INTR at instruction boundaries with the INTA handshake, NMI on
its edge and blocked until IRET, the shadow after MOV SS, POP SS and STI, and
HLT waiting for either. It is stacked on [the I/O slice](2026-10-27-i386-io.md).

Run data: [`2026-10-27-i386-interrupts.jsonl`](2026-10-27-i386-interrupts.jsonl).
- The checks are of `3fb5ddce36`. The branch has merged the I/O branch since
  that took #1170's fix (count-zero REP strings in D, two directed cases).
- The routes are of `6df0ef6d7d`, whose RTL is `3fb5ddce36`'s. The before
  figures are a route of the I/O branch with that fix merged
  (`7302669c6a`), so both sides have it.
- Two earlier routes of this slice, `385b07bb38` and `7909db986a`, are in
  the run data as `first` and `second`. They found the two costs fixed here
  (see Result).
- Eight nextpnr seeds. A Quartus row's `fingerprint` is the first 12 hex
  digits of the SHA-256 of its `sources` map, as JSON with sorted keys.

## The lines

| Port | Direction | Contract |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `intr` | In | A level the machine's PICs hold until INTA |
| `nmi` | In | Taken on its rising edge |
| `inta_req` | Out | The CPU takes INTR and asks for its vector, until ... |
| `inta_ack`, `inta_vector` | In | ... the machine returns it, on this edge |

## How it runs

- **When.** On the edge an instruction's last uop retires, a delivery's
  included, or while the CPU is in HLT.
  - NMI comes first, unless one is being handled: an NMI blocks the next
    until IRET's last uop retires. A new microcode bit, `iret`, marks it.
  - INTR is taken if IF is set after the instruction.
  - Neither is taken after MOV SS or POP SS, unless that one follows
    another, nor after an STI that set IF. That is the shadow as Bochs keeps
    it. LSS has none.
- **How.** The decision is a register, `int_take`, loaded on that edge.
  - An INTR first asks for its vector: `inta_req` until `inta_ack`, with D
    to WB frozen. That wait is one register, `int_wait`, set at the boundary
    and cleared on the acknowledging edge.
  - The I/O slice's wait is one register now too (`io_wait`, in place of
    `io_wb` and `io_done`). So the freeze, which every stage's enable takes,
    stays five inputs, one LUT.
  - Then WB flushes, as for a fault. The uop in WB, the next instruction's,
    neither retires, stores nor makes an I/O access. EX's running flags go
    back to EFLAGS, since EX has run that uop.
  - DC takes the vector routine's entry with D's EIP the next instruction's,
    so the routine pushes it. An NMI's vector is 2.
- **HLT** waits for an interrupt. `trace_halt` is still HLT or the stop for
  good, which `trace_unimplemented` tells apart, so a testbench that knows
  no interrupts runs as before. A HLT that retires with an interrupt pending
  takes it at once. `halted`, which D's issue reads, stays one register: set
  with either, cleared as an interrupt wakes HLT.
- **Not built yet:** interrupts between a REP string's elements. A REP runs
  to its end first, which the oracle's step of a whole REP cannot check
  without a per-iteration hook. That is the follow-up.

## The testbench

Random programs take interrupts now. Corpus states take none.
- **A PIC per side** (`Pic`): INTR, held until INTA returns the vector and
  drops it, and NMI. The RTL's INTA completes after the same random 0-12
  cycles as its other ports. Each step must make as many INTAs on each side,
  each with INTR up.
- **Alignment.** The RTL samples INTR and NMI as an instruction retires; the
  oracle before the next one starts. So an interrupt meant for the boundary
  after a step reaches the RTL before its window for that step, and the
  oracle before the next step.
  - Both then take it at the same boundary, or both wait (IF clear, the
    shadow, NMI blocked) and look again at every boundary after.
  - The RTL's NMI is a one-cycle pulse. It is never raised on two steps in a
    row, so the line is low between pulses.
  - No line is raised for the next step while the RTL is in HLT: its wake
    is this step's, and a line raised now would meet it at the same
    boundary.
- **What is raised.**
  - INTR now and then, on vectors 8-15. It comes one step in two after STI,
    CLI, MOV SS, POP SS, POPF and IRET, whose boundaries are special, and one
    in 32 otherwise.
  - NMI one step in 8 after those and after the handler's NOP, where it
    meets the NMI being handled, and one in 256 otherwise.
  - The vectors' entries and NMI's point at a handler, NOP and IRET, at
    FFFF:FFE0, above any program's code.
- **New blocks in the generator.**
  - STI, HLT: a HLT before the program's last, with IF set, wakes on INTR.
  - The shadow's cases back to back: MOV SS twice, POP SS twice, MOV SS then
    STI, CLI then STI twice, STI then POP SS, with SS reloaded with its own
    value.
- **A step that takes an interrupt runs no instruction.** So only the
  divergence entries for every opcode apply to it. That is
  `interrupt-vector-before-push`, when the frame lands on the vector's own
  entry: the RTL reads the entry first, as the 386EX does, and Bochs after
  pushing. A fault Bochs's step then takes at the stale address is part of
  that step.
- **A program may write over an entry or the handler.** The run stops, before
  the sides part, when an interrupt would go through one
  (`vector_moved_stops`): what runs next is data.
- **Per program** the oracle is reset before its state is set, since
  `set_state()` keeps a pending or blocked NMI. Each program's interrupts
  come from their own seed, so `FES_I386_FIRST` reruns them.

**The slice costs nothing measurable.** nextpnr averages 64.3 MHz against
63.3, Quartus 89.9 against 88.2, every stage within its seeds' spread.
Quartus's worst path is still the multiplier in logic, as on the I/O slice.

**Two earlier routes each found a cost, and both are fixed here.** Neither
had #1170's fix, so their before is the I/O slice's own route (64.4).

| Route | nextpnr average | Limited by | The D paths |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First, `385b07bb38` | 61.3 | EX ×4, D ×2, M2, DC | `md_busy` through the freeze into D's issue, to the string engine's count registers |
| Second, `7909db986a` | 61.4 | D ×6, EX ×2 | the same from `int_wait` or `md_busy`, and from `hlt` through `halted` |

- **The freeze.** INTA's wait was `int_take && !int_nmi && !inta_done`, which
  made the freeze eight inputs, two LUT levels. Every stage's enable and D's
  issue take it. One register per wait (`int_wait`, and I/O's `io_wait` for
  `io_wb && !io_done`) brings it back to five inputs, one LUT.
- **`halted`.** Split into HLT and the stop for good, it was an OR of two
  registers on D's issue. It is one register again.

## What the design doc should say

This is reported for the doc's owner to write; the doc is unchanged here.

- **The sampling point** is the edge an instruction's last uop retires,
  with IF and the shadow as they are after it, and HLT samples every cycle.
- **NMI's block** ends when IRET's last uop retires. Bochs unmasks NMI as
  IRET starts, so an IRET that faults leaves NMI unblocked there and blocked
  here. No check has met that case yet.
