# i386 seq holds: every rewritten field set at every step

Dated record, 2026-10-27. Host simulation and routed diagnostics on Powerboat.
No package, RBF or kit.

After [the issue cut](2026-10-27-i386-issue.md), DC still limited four of
eight nextpnr seeds. Part of the cause was `fes_i386_seq`: each step kept the
fields it did not change from `dc` itself. A field that a sequence rewrites
at some steps and keeps at others therefore had the step's condition in its
enable, and Yosys built that after D's issue. This slice sets every such
field outright at every step instead. It is a timing cut only, with no cycle
changed, which the latency-free comparison below shows.

Run data: [`2026-10-27-i386-seqhold.jsonl`](2026-10-27-i386-seqhold.jsonl).
- The before figures are the issue cut's `36a3567502` (its record's rows).
- Routes and the full lockstep runs are of `670cc7ac93`. The README commit
  after it changes no RTL.
- Eight nextpnr seeds. A Quartus row's `fingerprint` is the first 12 hex
  digits of the SHA-256 of its `sources` map, as JSON with sorted keys.

## The change

- **`dc0`** keeps the instruction's first uop. It loads with `dc_load`, as
  `dc`'s other first-uop fields do.
- **`fes_i386_seq`** works out each step's uop from `dc0` and the step:
  - the uop at step k has every change of the steps before it applied
    again;
  - the per-step controls (`more`, `reads_seg`, `push_eip`, `far_t`, ...)
    are reset at every step, as before;
  - from `dc` it reads only fields no step rewrites (`seq`, `opsize32`, the
    `m_*` operand fields) and the counters (`step`, `upc`).
- **In microcode**, `mem.seg` is SS for a stack access, else the first uop's.
  The vector table's reads come before any stack access in every routine.
  The far jump after them has no memory operand, and a far jump's AG sum is
  32-bit, so the segment's high half it loads is not used.
- **`upc`** counts at every step; only `SEQ_UCODE` reads it.

`dc` now loads on two enables from `issue`:
- the 136 rewritten bits on `!dc_valid || issue` (one LUT, then an
  inverter);
- the 78 first-uop bits, with `dc0`'s 103, on `dc_load`, two LUTs.

| Netlist (Yosys `238eac9a49`) | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `dc`'s enables, LUTs after `issue` | 2-5 (16 bits at 4 or 5: `mem.seg`, `seg_index`, the address fields, `src_size`, `in_a`) | 1-2 |
| `dc_valid`, `dc_skid_valid` | 3 | 3 |
| `issue` | 5 LUT levels | 6 (ABC's mapping, not this change) |

## What the design doc should say

This is reported for the doc's owner to write; the doc is unchanged here.

- **Fixed sequences are cumulative.** Each step's uop is the first uop with
  every step's changes so far, so no field of `dc` is held at some steps
  and not others.
- **DC's buffer has two enables:** a sequence's rewritten fields at every
  issue, and the instruction's own fields at its last.
- **DC is no longer the CPU's usual limit on nextpnr.** EX is, with a depth
  that moves between 10 and 11 LUT levels as ABC maps unrelated builds, and
  Q's prefix loop after it. Either is the next cut. EX's would make its
  result and flags paths depend less on that mapping.
- **What remains of DC's loop** is `dc_load` (built on `!dc_valid || issue`)
  into `dc_valid` and `dc_skid_valid`.
