# i386 DAA, DAS, AAA and AAS

Dated record, 2026-10-28. Host simulation and routed diagnostics on Powerboat.
No package, RBF or kit.

This slice builds DAA, DAS, AAA and AAS (`27`, `2F`, `37`, `3F`), each one
uop in EX, as the design's [execute](../i386-cpu-design.md#execute) section
asks. It is stacked on [the bit test and scan slice](2026-10-28-i386-bits.md).

Run data: [`2026-10-28-i386-bcd.jsonl`](2026-10-28-i386-bcd.jsonl).

## The 386EX's flags

The manual leaves OF undefined after DAA and DAS, and SF, ZF, PF and OF
after AAA and AAS. The Phase 0 divergence entries `daa-das-of` and
`aaa-aas-flags` already follow the 386EX, so the corpus run checks them
against SingleStepTests' states. Before any RTL, the rules below were fitted
to the four files and checked on every state: all 10,000 match. None faults.

In the table, lo is AL's low digit above 9 or AF set, and hi AL above 99h or
CF set.

| Instruction | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| DAA | AL plus 6 if lo, plus 60h if hi. AF is lo, CF hi. SF, ZF and PF of the new AL; OF its signed overflow: the old AL's top bit clear and the new one's set |
| DAS | AL less 6 if lo, less 60h if hi. AF is lo; CF is hi or the borrow out of AL less 6 (AF set with AL below 6). SF, ZF and PF of the new AL; OF the old top bit set and the new one clear |
| AAA | AX plus 106h if lo, so AH takes AL plus 6's carry as well as 1; then AL's high digit is cleared. CF and AF are lo. SF, ZF, PF and OF of AL plus 6 (or of AL) before the clear, OF as DAA's |
| AAS | AX less 6, then AH less 1, if lo; then AL's high digit is cleared. CF and AF are lo. SF, ZF, PF and OF of AL less 6 (or of AL) before the clear, OF as DAS's |

Rules a fit has to tell apart are well covered. The 8086's AAA and AAS,
whose AH takes 1 and not AL's carry or borrow, fail 210 and 169 states. 190
AAA states have a cleared byte of 0 where AL plus 6 is not, so ZF from the
result would fail them; 170 DAS states have AF set with AL below 6, where
the borrow sets CF; 37 DAA and 38 AAA states have AL 7Ah to 7Fh, where the
add overflows.

LOCK raises #UD on all four, as on any instruction that is not a
read-modify-write.

## How it runs

- **Decode.** Column 7 of rows 2 and 3 of the one-byte map, beside the
  segment PUSH and POP of rows 0 and 1. EAX is src1 and the destination:
  8 bits for DAA and DAS, 16 for AAA and AAS. All four read AF, and DAA and
  DAS CF, from EX's running copy, so DC marks them flag consumers and D
  holds them a cycle behind a shift, as it holds ADC.
- **M2** registers three controls: the adjust, a subtract (DAS, AAS) and the
  ASCII forms (AAA, AAS).
- **EX** computes the adjust beside the adder, from x (AL or AX) after the
  bypass. Each digit's sum is a table, entry d of a 64-bit constant for the
  digit plus or less 1, 6 and 7, and the compares (above 9, above 99h, below
  6) are spelled out, so no `+`, `-` or `<` reaches Yosys there and no carry
  chain is added to EX. AH's step of 1 or 2 up or down is an increment by
  prefix ANDs of AH, inverted for AAS. The result goes through the other
  results' OR; the flags take the multiply unit's select in EX's flag chain
  (below).

## The testbench

- `SCOPE` gains the four stems, so the corpus run takes 917 files.
- Random programs gain DAA, DAS, AAA and AAS, half of them right after an
  ADD, ADC, SUB or SBB into AL, so AL comes from the WB register and AF and
  CF from the uop just ahead.
- The lockstep's flag-reader list for its hold check (`consumes_flags`)
  gains the four. A first run without it failed the hold check: 20 adjusts
  right after a shift retired two cycles after it, where the check wanted
  one. That is D's hold working; the check now expects two.
- In random programs the divergent flags are left to the corpus, so there
  the oracle takes the RTL's.

## Netlist depth

The base's EX is 12 LUT levels and 1 carry cell, on the multiply unit's path
to `md_flags`. Synthesizing as `scripts/benchmark_fes_i386.py` does, with
Yosys alone before any route, every stage stays at or below the base's
deepest path:

| Stage | Q | DC | D | AG | M2 | EX | WB | L2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Base `592be15ebf` | 9 | 12 | 11 | 5 + 29 carry | 12 | 12 + 1 carry | 8 | 11 |
| Adjusts `e5334c537a` | 10 | 12 | 11 | 5 + 29 carry | 12 | 12 + 1 carry | 7 | 9 |

Q and L2 move with ABC's mapping of the whole netlist; neither is touched.

**The flags' place in EX's chain.** EX's flags are a chain of `if`s, one per
source, and each adds a select in front of every source before it: EFLAGS
before the uop, which comes through WB's shift flags, and the logic unit's
ZF, an OR of 32 bits. The adjusts' flags first had an `if` of their own,
before the bit test's. EX was still 12 levels and 1 carry cell, but the EX
endpoints at 12 levels doubled, from 12 to 24, three of them flip-flops of
EX's running EFLAGS against the base's one. Two other placements,
synthesized alike:

| The adjusts' flags | EX | EX endpoints at 12 levels | Yosys ALUTs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Base, none | 12 + 1 carry | 12 | 18,827 |
| Their own `if`, before the bit test's | 12 + 1 carry | 24 | 18,850 |
| Their own `if`, last | 12 + 1 carry | 20 | 18,829 |
| The multiply unit's `if`, each flag a select between the two (built) | 12 + 1 carry | 17 | 18,876 |

The built one adds no select to the chain: the multiply unit's flags are
registers, so the select between them and the adjusts' costs the adjusts one
level and nothing else. Between the three the counts are within ABC's noise,
so it is chosen for its structure.

**The nextpnr drop is not measured against a fair base.**
- **The paths.** Five of the head's eight critical paths are in stages this
  slice doesn't touch:
  - D on seeds 1, 3 and 6, from `int_wait` through the data forward to
    `r_src` or `r_dst`; D on seed 4, `dops.ready` to `s_kill1`.
  - AG's 29-carry address add on seed 5.
  - The three EX ones run from EX's running flags through its result to WB,
    as the base's EX-limited seeds did. Two of them take the adder's carry
    chain, which the adjusts don't use.
- **The other stages.** DC, D and AG fall by 5 to 6 MHz, as EX does, and
  L1 and M1 by 12 to 20. L1 is 88-100 MHz on every base seed and 67-79 MHz
  on every head seed. Q and WB stay level.
- **The load.** The base was routed before the bit test and scan record's
  commit at 11:58. The head was routed from 13:30 to 16:40, beside other
  sessions' i386 routes.
  - The head took 697-8,141 s per seed against the base's 369-647 s. Its

  - All seven head logs that survive stopped the analogue repair at its
    300-second wall-clock budget (`gpurouter/analogueTimeBudget`), two of
    them while still gaining. The budget leaves out lease waits but not a
    shared GPU or a loaded host.
  - The base's logs are gone, so whether its repairs stopped early is not
    known.
- **The load moves the result.** The benchmark routed seed 4 again at
  16:40, on the same `synth.json` with the same command, on a quieter host.
  - Placement took 98 s.
  - The analogue repair ran 9 rounds against 4 or 5 and ended inside its
    budget.
  - The seed reached 58.47 MHz against 56.83, with a different critical
    path.
  - Seeds 2 and 3, routed twice under the afternoon's load, matched.
  - So the head's figures are lowered by the load, by an amount that a set
    of routes all made under one load can't separate from the RTL.
- **Seed 1.** The main route converged at iteration 43. Its timing repair's

## What the design doc should say

This is reported for the doc's owner to write; the doc is unchanged here.

- **The microcode list** names "BCD adjust" among the microcode routines.
  DAA, DAS, AAA and AAS are one uop each in EX, as the execute section's BCD
  line already says; the microcode list should drop them.
- **The BCD line's** "the i386's undefined-flag results" are the 386EX's,
  tabled above: SF, ZF and PF of the byte the adjust adds or subtracts before
  AAA and AAS clear its high digit, and OF its signed overflow.
