# i386 EX cut 2: operands from M2, a second carry chain, shift CF and OF at WB

Dated record, 2026-10-26. Host simulation and routed diagnostics on Powerboat.
No package, RBF or kit.

After the [D cut](2026-10-25-i386-d-cut.md), EX limited the whole CPU on
nextpnr. This cut takes EX's operand preparation, its shift flags and its
size selects off EX's paths, with no added cycle. It also takes on the
adder's half of rule 8, which the [first EX cut](2026-10-25-i386-ex-cut.md)
left undone. Both flows were re-routed, seeds 1-4:
- nextpnr `bd7a1c0d` through `gpu-run`, HIP, with `NEXTPNR_REQUIRE_GPU=1` and
  an 80 MHz target, from a `git archive` of each commit;
- Quartus 17.0.2 through `scripts/quartus_fes_i386.py --dsp-in-logic` at
  100 MHz.

Run data: [`2026-10-26-i386-ex-cut2.jsonl`](2026-10-26-i386-ex-cut2.jsonl).
- **nextpnr before.** These rows are the base, `1f5ee01720` (the D cut's
  branch head), re-routed here. The D cut's own rows (`8f54d8015a`) came from
  the benchmark script before its Q label change, so their fingerprint
  differs. The netlist is the same, and the re-route reproduced their Fmax
  and per-stage figures exactly.
- **Quartus before.** These are the D cut's rows, fingerprint
  `92ee4a097135`. That fingerprint covers only the RTL and spike sources, and
  those are unchanged at this base, so the rows were not re-fitted.
- **After.** Every row is `b89513835f`. An earlier route of the RTL commit
  `97ed363071`, which differs only in comments and in the shift-flag module's
  file, gave the same Fmax on every seed.
- **Fingerprints.** As in the D cut: a nextpnr row carries the benchmark's
  source fingerprint, and a Quartus row the first 12 hex digits of the
  SHA-256 of its `sources` map.

The RTL still carries the DeanoC/yosys#59 workaround in qalign.

## The path

On the base every seed's EX path ran from EX's registered controls
(`ex.exc`) to `wb_s`, at 9-11 cells and 64-67% routing. In the base's netlist
the deepest EX endpoints are `wb_s.result`, `wb_s.sh_cf`, `wb_s.sh_of` and ZF
into `ex_flags`, all at 11 LUT levels. They come from:
1. the bypass and the byte extract (`byp_a`, `size8`, `src1.hi8`): 2 levels;
2. the shifter's arrangement: 3;
3. the shift: 5;
4. the result select and the lane merge, or the shift's CF and OF selects:
   2-3.

The routed paths' signal list names `ex.def_co`, but that is a cell name:
`def_co`'s own cone is 4 levels deep. The adder's CF and OF map at 4 levels
before the carry chain and 2-3 after it.

## The cut

1. **M2 prepares EX's operands** (`fes_i386_exctl`, into `x` and `y` in the
   pipeline record).
   - x is src1 and y is src2 or what the operation adds (the immediate, 1
     for INC, DEC and LOOP, the size's ones for NOT, src1 for NEG).
   - Each is cut to the operation's size, with a high byte moved down; a
     MOVZX, MOVSX or SIGN source is cut to its own size.
   - y is inverted for a subtract.
   - Above an add's size, x is ones and y zeros, so its carry out is the
     chain's bit 32 at every size. The post-chain size select is gone.
   - An operand still pending with its producer one stage ahead is left out.
     EX ORs it into x, or XORs it into y (the inversion stays in), from the
     WB result register through registered selects: bits 7:0 or 15:8 into
     the low byte, and 15:8 and 31:16 in place. That is one LUT level from
     the registers.
   - The logic unit, the shifter, MOV and the extends all read x and y. NOT
     becomes an XOR with the size's ones.
2. **A second carry chain, in parallel, gives OF and SF.**
   - It adds the same operands with the size's top bit filled the same way.
     Its carry out is the carry into the top bit.
   - OF is the two chains' carries XORed. SF is that carry XORed with the
     top bit's propagate, which comes from x and y and is ready early.
   - Neither needs a size select after a chain. Rule 2 forbids dependent
     chains; these two are side by side.
3. **The shift's CF and OF move to WB** (`fes_i386_shflags`).
   - EX hands WB the bit shifted out at the right, the one out at the left
     for the size, and an 8-bit SHL or SHR's CF at a count of 16 or 24. WB
     takes CF and OF from those and the merged result.
   - The flags already reached EX's running copy from WB a cycle late.
     D's existing hold (one cycle for a flag consumer behind a shift) covers
     this, so nothing new waits.
4. **The result is one OR per lane.** The sum, the shift, the other results
   and the old value each enter it under a registered lane select: bits 7:0,
   15:8, the low byte's result into 15:8 for a high-byte destination, and
   31:16.
   - The early-LEA mux after EX is gone. A 32-bit LEA's offset is its whole
     result, which the LEA select passes.
5. **Each adder flag takes its chain bit through one gate**, beside a value
   worked out without the chain (the logic unit's, a flag instruction's, or
   the old flag).

The shifter itself is unchanged. A radix-4 version with kept stage wires was
tried in an EX-alone probe: the shift became 3 levels, but ABC moved logic
from the arrangement in front of it and the total stayed at 8-9.

## Rule 8

The deepest path through a carry chain to each of EX's flag and result
registers, as LUT levels before the chain and after it:

| Endpoint | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CF into `wb_s.eflags` | 4 / 3 | 1 / 1 |
| OF into `wb_s.eflags` | 4 / 2 | 2 / 1 |
| ZF into `wb_s.eflags` | 4 / 2 | 1 / 1 |
| SF into `wb_s.eflags` | 4 / 2 | 2 / 2 |
| AF into `wb_s.eflags` | 4 / 2 | 1 / 2 |
| PF into `wb_s.eflags` | 4 / 3 | 1 / 3 |
| CF into `ex_flags` | 4 / 3 | 1 / 2 |
| OF into `ex_flags` | 4 / 3 | 2 / 2 |
| `wb_s.result` | 4 / 3 | 1 / 3 |

- **CF, OF and ZF meet rule 8 into the pipeline register:** two levels or
  fewer before the chain and one after.
- **Into EX's running flags they take one more level.** That is the
  register's own load. In the final netlist the last LUT in front of
  `ex_flags` CF and OF takes `init_eflags` and the reset beside the flag:
  the reset value is not a constant.
  Removing the hold for an empty or flushed cycle (the flag selects gated by
  `live`, `ex_flags` loaded every cycle) was tried and left CF and OF at 2.
  It was not kept.
- **Not met: SF and AF** (2 after), **PF** (3 after: an 8-bit parity of the
  sum), and **the result's 15:8 lane and some bits of 31:16** (up to 3
  after).
  - The 15:8 lane takes two sums, two shifts and the other results under
    nine inputs, which no one LUT can take.
  - ABC9 maps those paths deeper than they need to be because the shift
    path, at 9 levels, sets the stage's depth.
  - None of them is near EX's routed limit.

## What the design doc should say

This is reported for the doc's owner to write; the doc is unchanged here.

- **M2 prepares EX's operands.**
  - The Execute section should say that EX's operands arrive cut to size, a
    subtrahend inverted, and an add's operands filled above its size.
  - Only an operand whose producer is one stage ahead joins in EX, one LUT
    level from the WB register.
  - When loads arrive, load data meets EX the same way: aligned and
    extended, it should join x or y through a select like the WB bypass, not
    through a mux in front of the operand preparation.
- **The late ALU has two carry chains,** the sum and a parallel one for the
  carry into the top bit (OF and SF). Rule 2 permits that, since they do not
  depend on each other.
- **Rule 8.**
  - It holds for CF, OF and ZF into the pipeline register: at most two
    levels before the chain and one after.
  - SF, AF, PF and the result's middle byte are 2-3 levels after it.
  - The running-flags register adds one level for its reset load.
- **The shift's CF and OF are applied at WB** with its other flags. The
  latency row for a flag consumer after a shift is unchanged.
- **Clock.**
  - Quartus closes the whole CPU at 102.8-106.1 MHz, Q-limited, which meets
    the 100 MHz design point on every seed.
  - nextpnr closes it at 71.8-83.3, limited by D and Q, behind the
    place-and-route gap DeanoC/nextpnr#300 records.
  - EX is no longer the first cut either flow needs.
