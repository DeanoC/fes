# i386 self-modifying code in flight

Dated record, 2026-10-29. Host simulation, Quartus fits and routed
diagnostics on Powerboat and the CUDA route host. No package, RBF or kit.

The second [memory slice](../i386-cpu-design.md#memory-slices): every store
the CPU commits, and every DMA write, is checked against the bytes the front
end has already fetched, under the two
[self-modifying-code profiles](../i386-cpu-design.md#self-modifying-code).
It is stacked on the first memory slice
([#1230](https://github.com/DeanoC/fes/pull/1230), `8c7011fd26`), and
closes the window that slice left: a fetch in F2 the cycle after a store's
commit, before the L1I's clear lands.

Run data: [`2026-10-29-i386-smc.jsonl`](2026-10-29-i386-smc.jsonl): the
lockstep runs, the mutations, the synthesis of slice 1 (`before`,
`c4b9663d64`) and of this slice (`after`, `409186807c`), the Quartus fits of
both, the nextpnr routes and the boot. A Quartus row's `flow` is `quartus`,
in the shape of slice 1's record's.

**Result:** the window is closed. A store or DMA write into bytes the front
end holds restarts the instruction that would run them, under `coherent` the
next one and under `queue16` the first past the 16-byte window. RTL and
netlist lockstep pass on 1,734,099 corpus states under `queue16` and
32,000 random programs under `coherent`. In the random programs, 17,931
restarts happen and no instruction runs a stale byte. Slice 1's RTL fails 69
of 300 of the new random programs. FreeDOS boots on the RTL CPU with 620,366
restarts, 1.3% fewer instructions in its fixed time. The slice adds 247 LUTs,
218 carry cells and 48 flip-flops, and keeps the freeze and every stage's
enable at slice 1's depth. Quartus 17.0.2, the timing reference, fits it
at 100.1 MHz on average against slice 1's 100.3, the same within the seed
spread. The check's registers are 15 MHz or more clear of every seed's worst
path ([Quartus](#quartus)). nextpnr's four seeds, kept as data, are 38.1 to
44.8 MHz, against slice 1's 47.5 to 48.7. The L1I's own path limits three of
them.

## What the slice builds

**The bytes in flight.** Fetch is sequential from the last redirect, with no
prediction, and every redirect comes from WB. So the instructions after the
one in WB are one run of bytes: from CS.base + EIP to the end of the block
F1 loads next (`fetch_next`, the fetch buffer's next block address, now a
port of the front end). That block counts because F2 reads it while the L1I
still holds a line a store has just committed into: the L1I clears the line
on the edge after the commit. The CPU takes the run's lower end from
`start_linear`, CS.base + EIP a cycle old, which may start it an instruction
early.

**The check.** A store WB commits, or a DMA write (the bus interface's
`inv_valid`), compares its physical line, and a store's second line when its
lanes cross into one, with that run. Lines compare by bits 19:5, modulo
1 MiB, where linear and physical addresses agree under A20M and for the
shadow at FFFF0000h. The compare is two carry chains: the line's distance
from the run's start, subtracted from the run's length, a borrow meaning a
miss. Written as a `<=`, Yosys gave the second compare five LUT levels.
Every input is a register, and the decision reaches only registers.

**The restart.** An instruction after the store restarts: its first uop takes
`FAULT_REPLAY` as it enters WB, WB flushes it and everything younger, and the
front end restarts at it through `start`, the uncached load's replay path,
without serial issue. That uop has passed EX, so EX's running flags go back
to EFLAGS, as for an interrupt. By the time the front end refetches, the L1I
has cleared the line, and the fill waits for the store's write-buffer entry,
so the instruction runs the new bytes. The instruction that stored completes
as it was decoded, as the 386EX does; so does an instruction already in WB
when a DMA write lands.

**Which instruction.**
- **`coherent`**, the default, and every DMA write: the next instruction,
  once the instruction in WB ends (`smc_pend` until it does, `smc_due`
  after).
- **`queue16`**: the 16 bytes after the storing instruction keep what the
  front end fetched. A store with no byte past them needs no restart, which
  takes in a store behind them, over the storing instruction's own encoding,
  where nothing younger is. A store with a byte past them restarts the first
  instruction to reach past them (`smc_defer`, the window's end in `smc_t`),
  checked as each instruction's first uop enters WB, as a 386 refetches once
  it runs past its queue. So a HLT within the window still halts: the
  corpus's 386EX does, after a REP MOVS or STOS over its own encoding and
  ~100 bytes after it.

The first version of `queue16` restarted the next instruction for any store
past the window, as `coherent` does. Four corpus states failed with it,
all of them the
[divergence entry](../../cores/fes-common/sim/i386/divergences.toml)
`rep-overwrites-own-encoding`. The window's end is the fix.

**Uncached loads.** An uncached load reads its device only as the oldest uop.
While a restart is due (`smc_due`), or `queue16` waits for an instruction
past its window (`smc_defer`), the load replays instead: the restart would
make it read again. The random programs found this. With an uncached load in
the next instruction, the restart made the device see two reads where the
oracle made one. A replay is a flush, and ends `queue16`'s wait as a jump
does.

**The profile** is a new CPU input, `queue16`, sampled at reset.
`trace_smc` marks each restart; `trace_replay` stays the uncached loads'.

### Not built in this slice

- With branch prediction, the run of bytes in flight is no longer one
  stretch, and the check needs the predicted path's lines.
- With the L2, DMA writes into code take the same check and the L2's
  back-invalidation.

Known and left:
- A store into a line of data next to code in flight restarts for nothing.
  So do an alias 1 MiB away, and a run a backward redirect leaves wrapped for
  a cycle. FreeDOS pays for the first ([FreeDOS](#freedos)).
- `queue16` is no exact 386:
  - a byte the front end has not fetched yet reads new;
  - an instruction that starts in the window and ends past it restarts;
  - DMA restarts the next instruction, as under `coherent`.
- The A20M note from slice 1 stands: a store takes its physical address as
  it enters WB.

## The testbench

The lockstep's random programs stopped at any store into their own code
before, since the RTL had no check: 83 of 32,000 in slice 1's record.

**Stale bytes.** `Rtl::stale` watches the fetch and store traces. It keeps
the bytes a retired store wrote into blocks the front end has fetched since
it last fetched out of sequence, at or after the storing instruction, and a
block fetched on the store's own edge counts. An instruction other than the
storing one that retires over such a byte ran bytes from before the store,
and fails the step. That holds under `coherent` only; the corpus runs under
`queue16`.

**Random programs** now and then store through CS into a later instruction:
- one or both bytes of MOV r16, imm16's or ALU AX, imm16's immediate;
- MOV r8, imm8's immediate;
- a one-byte instruction, rewritten as another.

The target is the next instruction, one after up to three others, or one
14 to 33 bytes of others on. A run no longer stops at a store into its code,
unless the store is not one of those rewrites: after that, the program may
run anything. Gaps after a restart go to their own histogram, `smc`, so the
latency-free hold checks stay exact.

**Directed cases**, 22 new:
- Ten under `coherent` against Bochs, from CS:0:
  - a store into the next instruction's immediate;
  - over a NOP;
  - 20 bytes ahead;
  - a word across a line;
  - before a taken JMP;
  - by PUSH, with the stack in the code;
  - by REP STOSB over the instructions after it;
  - over the storing instruction's own bytes;
  - through DS;
  - in a loop that increments the immediate it adds.
- Six on the RTL alone, each run under both profiles, checking one register:
  - a store into the next instruction;
  - the 16th byte after the storing one;
  - the 17th;
  - a word over both;
  - REP STOSW over its own encoding and the next instruction;
  - REP STOSB over the HLT after it and 22 bytes on.

**A memory case** sweeps a DMA write over the immediate of MOV BL, 1, which
the front end fetched long before, behind six DIVs, at 100 cycles. The MOV
must run the DMA's byte when the write is done two cycles or more before it
retires, and the old one when after; some write must restart it.

**The corpus runs under `queue16`**: it is a 386EX's, with its prefetch
queue.

The two sites are FreeDOS kernel instructions at 99EDAh and 12C0Eh. Each

same figures: that commit differs only in `queue16`'s window.

### Quartus

slice 1's ([Quartus](2026-10-28-i386-memory-path.md#quartus)):
`scripts/quartus_fes_i386.py` at `06079439c2`

**In Quartus the slice costs nothing measurable:** its average is 0.2 MHz
lower, against slice 1's seed spread of 1.6 MHz.
- **The check is far from every seed's worst path.**
  - Its registers' worst paths are at 115 MHz or more on every seed:
    `smc_due`, `wb_smc`, `wb_q16`, `smc_pend`, `smc_chk` and `smc_defer`.
  - `wb_next_lin`, `smc_t` and `q16` are at 190 MHz or more.
  - WB's decisions, which the restart also feeds, reach 104.9 to 106.7 MHz
    (`wb_redir`) and 106.4 to 110.4 (`wb_flushes`). On slice 1's seed 1,
    `wb_flushes` limited the CPU, at 99.85.
- **F2 limits seeds 1 and 3**, at 99.96 and 98.72 MHz, through the L1I hit
  path that limits slice 1's seed 3 in F1. The path runs from `l1i.blk`
  through the valid-bit select to `l1i.hit` and `f2_advance`, into F2's
  `l1_bytes` or `l1_valid`. It has 6 or 7 levels, with 5.7 to 6.1 ns of
  interconnect, and the slice does not change it.
  - Without F1 and F2, the four seeds would be limited at 101.60, 100.40,
    101.96 and 101.14 MHz, 101.3 on average. So the registered-valid-bits
    cut that slice 1's record describes
    ([The cuts](2026-10-28-i386-memory-path.md#the-cuts)) would be worth
    about a MHz here.
- **D limits seed 2**, at 100.40 MHz: D's decode, from `decode.by` through
  `sib_form`. **Q limits seed 4**, at 101.14: the aligner.
- **Size.** The slice adds about 95 ALMs, 110 registers and a RAM block.

### Depth before placing

Slice 1's lesson was to keep the freeze one LUT of registers and to count
LUT levels on a fresh synthesis before placing. The check stays off the
freeze and every stage's enable: the enables of D to WB are 3 levels or
fewer, as before. What it feeds is WB's entry: the fault field and taken
bit of the uop entering WB, and the registered decisions WB acts on.

| Register (D input) | Slice 1 | First version | This slice |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `wb_flushes` | 9 (EX) | 11.15 (the line compare) | 9 (EX) |
| `wb_redir` | 10 (EX) | 11.15 (the line compare) | 10 (EX) |
| `smc_due`, `wb_smc` | — | 11.15 | 9 |
| `smc_pend` | — | 10.15 | 8 |
| `wb_q16` | — | 11 | 7.4 |

LUT levels from a register or M10K output, carries at 0.05 each, of the
synthesized netlists. The first version wrote the line compare as `<=`,
which Yosys made five LUT levels after a carry chain. As a subtraction's
borrow both halves are carry chains. A later version of the `queue16` check
added a level to `wb_redir` through the committing store. It was dropped:
the instruction right after a storing one starts in the window and is at
most 15 bytes long, so it cannot reach past it, and the check needs only
registers.

Elsewhere the deepest enables are the memory system's, as in slice 1. The
write buffer's data and the L1I's valid bits measure 11 or 12 levels, from
one synthesis to the next, on logic this slice does not touch.

Seeds 1, 2 and 4's critical paths are the L1I's own: from the fill's block
register or a valid bit, through the replacement choice and the request, into

- Seed 1's path has 10 LUTs and 22.7 ns: 4.7 ns of logic and 18.0 of wire.
- Seed 2's has 9 LUTs and 26.2 ns: 4.7 of logic and 21.5 of wire.
- Seed 4's has 10 LUTs and 23.1 ns: 4.7 of logic and 18.4 of wire.
- Slice 1's seed 4 was limited by a path of the same kind: 11 LUTs and

paths. On seed 1, the worst of the 114 nets named for the check (`smc_*`,

enable, at −6.97 and −8.14 ns, against −9.82 and −10.56. nextpnr's wire on
these paths belongs to the nextpnr project after bring-up
([DeanoC/fes#1235](https://github.com/DeanoC/fes/issues/1235), with
[DeanoC/nextpnr#300](https://github.com/DeanoC/nextpnr/issues/300)).

8,218 s for slice 1's seed 1. Seeds 3 and 4 took 19,023 and 19,695 s on
Powerboat, which routed two seeds of `21927e3f44` at the same time.

## Size

-nolutram`, of the routing spike's `spike_cpu`, whose stimulus now drives
`queue16` too.

The carry cells are the compares: three line compares of two 15-bit chains
each, the window's 20-bit offset and end, and the next instruction's linear
address. The flip-flops are the restart's state, the window's end and the
next instruction's address WB keeps.
