# i386 Quartus oracle: muldiv's products in DSP blocks, the rest in logic

Dated record, 2026-10-28. Host-only diagnostic on Powerboat. No package, RBF
or kit. Quartus is an oracle here.

[The Quartus-cores design](https://github.com/DeanoC/fes/blob/main/docs/design/quartus-cores.md)
(fes#1164, on main, not yet in this stack) ships a Quartus variant of a core
when, on the same RTL and clocks, Quartus's best Fmax beats the OSS flow's
best by 15% or more. It compares each flow at its best, so the two need not
put the same cells in DSP blocks. For i386, Quartus's figure comes from
`scripts/quartus_fes_i386.py`, which should give Quartus at its best. Since
[multiply and divide](2026-10-27-i386-muldiv.md), the CPU's multiplies can go
three ways in Quartus, and one is clearly the fastest:

| Multiplies in DSP blocks | Seeds 1-4, MHz | Average | Limited by | Commit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Quartus's choice: muldiv's four products and qalign's `dec[start]` stride, a 9×9 | 84.15, 86.41, 84.96, 85.57 | 85.3 | Q ×4, through the 9×9 | `f5de6b508e`, the muldiv record's |
| None (`--dsp-in-logic`) | 86.11, 85.42, 89.14, 91.22 | 88.0 | EX ×4, at the soft products | `4ef9e4da6d` |
| muldiv's four products only | 98.67, 99.53, 105.00, 101.01 | 101.1 | Q ×2, DC ×2 | `4ef9e4da6d` |

The script now builds only the last. It became the default at `4ef9e4da6d`,
which removed the mode where Quartus chose. This change removes
`--dsp-in-logic` as well: with each flow compared at its best, nothing needs
an all-logic run.

The first row is of the muldiv slice's RTL, not this one. On `4ef9e4da6d`,
`quartus_map` still infers the 9×9 when Quartus chooses (below), but no fits
of that mode ran here.

Run data: [`2026-10-28-i386-quartus-dsp.jsonl`](2026-10-28-i386-quartus-dsp.jsonl).
- The two 100 MHz sweeps are of `4ef9e4da6d`, whose RTL is the mid-REP
  interrupts slice's: `c14bf4eeac`'s, and `eeb56de165`'s, which
  [its record](2026-10-28-i386-rep-interrupts.md) routed.
- The 120 MHz sweep (below) is of `0c0da470c8`, after the flag was
  removed. Its RTL, fingerprint and marked products are the same.
- Seeds 1-4, at 100 MHz as in the earlier Quartus records, and the
  script's mapping again at 120 MHz.
- A 100 MHz row's `dsp_mode` is `oss` for the products in DSP blocks or
  `logic` for `--dsp-in-logic`. The script at `4ef9e4da6d` had both. The
  summary no longer has the field, and removing the flag leaves the
  default's netlist and Quartus settings as they were. The 120 MHz rows'
  state is `target-120`.
- A row's `dsp_products` lists the multiplies the script marked.
- A row's `fingerprint` is the first 12 hex digits of the SHA-256 of its
  `sources` map, as JSON with sorted keys.

## What each mapping puts in DSP blocks

The elaborated netlist (`read_verilog -sv; hierarchy; proc -norom; flatten;
opt -purge`) of the CPU spike has 14 multiplies:

| Multiply | Quartus's choice | The script | `--dsp-in-logic` (removed) | synth_intel_alm |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `fes_i386_muldiv`'s four 18×18 products, `p_ll` to `p_hh` | 4 DSP blocks | 4 DSP blocks | logic | 4 DSP blocks |
| qalign's `dec[start]` stride (`start * 21`, a 42-bit `lbyte_t`) | 1 DSP block, 9×9 | logic | logic | logic |
| 9 other constant multiplies (`* 3`, `* 5`) | logic | logic | logic | logic |

- **Quartus's choice.** Its map report: 5 DSP blocks, 4 for the products
  ("Two Independent 18x18") and one "Independent 9x9".
- **synth_intel_alm**, for comparison. Synthesized with the benchmark's
  recipe (`proc -norom; synth_intel_alm -family cyclonev -nolutram`) on the
  lock's Yosys `238eac9a49`, the CPU has 4 `MISTRAL_MUL18X18` cells, all
  `dut.muldiv`'s. The script's mapping matches it on this netlist. That is
  not why it is the script's: it is the fastest measured.

**The 9×9 is the stride multiply, not the byte rotate.** The muldiv record
says Quartus put qalign's byte rotate (`{source, source} >> {start,
3'b000}`) in it. It is the stride multiply beside it, in the same module:
- On every DSP seed in the muldiv record's rows, Q's worst path runs from
  `start` through `Mult0~mac` to `Mux719`. Quartus names a multiply
  `MultN` and a shift `ShiftRightN`.
- On this netlist, the map report infers `Mult0` from the line
  `start * 31'h15`, and the marks below land on `Mult1` to `Mult4`, the
  products.
- With the constant multiplies marked logic and Quartus otherwise free,
  Quartus uses 4 DSP blocks, not 5, and the extra unsigned multiplier is
  gone from its DSP summary. No shift went to a DSP block.

## How the script does it

- **DSP_BLOCK_BALANCING is logic elements**, so Quartus puts no multiply in
  a DSP block by its own choice.
- **Each product of two signals is marked** `(* multstyle = "dsp" *)` in
  the netlist, on its `*`. A multiply by a constant stays unmarked. Quartus
  takes the mark over the global setting: its map report lists
  `DSP_BLOCK_BALANCING = DSP blocks` as a source assignment on `Mult1` to
  `Mult4`, the four products, and puts them in DSP blocks.

So the DSP blocks a fit uses can only be the marked products. The script
marks four, every fit summary below has `Total DSP Blocks : 4 / 112`, and
every fit report's DSP Block Details lists `Mult1~mac` to `Mult4~mac`, each
"Two Independent 18x18". The four products are DSP blocks and nothing else
is. Seed 1's fit report:

| Name | Mode | Location | Sign Representation | Output Register |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mult1~mac | Two Independent 18x18 | DSP_X86_Y18_N0 | Unsigned | yes |
| Mult2~mac | Two Independent 18x18 | DSP_X86_Y14_N0 | Mixed | yes |
| Mult3~mac | Two Independent 18x18 | DSP_X86_Y16_N0 | Mixed | yes |
| Mult4~mac | Two Independent 18x18 | DSP_X86_Y12_N0 | Mixed | yes |

The rows' `netlist` field ends `write_verilog -noattr spike_cpu.v`, the
summary's wording at `4ef9e4da6d`. Yosys wrote `yosys.v` there too, and
`spike_cpu.v` is the marked copy Quartus reads; the script's summary now
names `yosys.v`.

Before settling on that, `quartus_map` alone ran on four versions of the
netlist:

| Netlist, Quartus setting | DSP blocks |
| --- | --- |
| Unmarked, Quartus's choice | 5: four 18×18 and one 9×9 |
| Products marked on the `*`, logic elements | 4 |
| Products' result wires marked, logic elements | 4 |
| The constant multiplies marked logic, Quartus's choice | 4 |

The first marked form is the script's. The last gives the same blocks on this
netlist, but leaves the choice to Quartus for anything not marked.

The rule is mechanical: a product of two signals goes to a DSP block, a
multiply by a constant to logic. It is the fastest of the three mappings
measured, Quartus's choice on the muldiv RTL and the other two on this one.
A later multiply may be faster the other way, a small product
of two signals in logic, say. The summary's `dsp_products` lists what was
marked.

## Result

| Quartus 17.0.2 (oracle), `4ef9e4da6d` | Products in DSP blocks (the script) | `--dsp-in-logic` (removed) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seeds 1-4 | 98.67, 99.53, 105.00, 101.01 | 86.11, 85.42, 89.14, 91.22 |
| Average | 101.1 | 88.0 |
| Limited by | Q ×2, DC ×2 | EX ×4 |
| Q, DC, D, EX averages | 101.5, 103.1, 107.3, 106.2 | 95.4, 96.4, 97.7, 88.0 |
| AG, M2, WB averages | 113.2, 114.4, 125.3 | 104.9, 104.5, 109.8 |
| DSP blocks | 4 on every seed | 0 |

**With the products in DSP blocks, Quartus fits this CPU at 101.1 MHz on
average, 13 MHz above all in logic.**
- **EX no longer limits.** It goes from 88.0 to 106.2. With every multiply
  in logic, every seed's EX worst path ends at a soft product (`p_hh` or
  `p_lh`). Now every seed's ends at `wb_s`, EX's own record.
- **Every stage is faster.** Q +6, DC +7, D +10, AG +8, M2 +10 and WB +15
  MHz on average, and no stage less, with about 590 fewer ALMs.
- **Q and DC limit now.** Q's worst path on seeds 1, 2 and 4 runs from
  `start` into D's byte register (`decode.by`). On seed 1, whose path is
  captured in full, it runs through the `dec[start]` stride, which Quartus
  now builds in logic (`Mult0_rtl_0`). DC's runs from `decode.by` through
  the decode.
- **Quartus also packs the products' output registers into the DSP
  blocks**, 130 register bits ("Output Register: yes"). synth_intel_alm's
  `MISTRAL_MUL18X18` is combinational, so the OSS netlist keeps them in
  ALMs. That is part of Quartus's best.
- **The all-logic figures are the mid-REP record's.** Its fits of
  `eeb56de165` have the same fingerprint, `bf749e66c1b2`, and the same
  Fmax and ALMs on every seed.

## A 120 MHz target

At 100 MHz, seeds 3 and 4 met the target, with worst slack +0.476 and
+0.100 ns. Seeds 1 and 2 missed it by 0.135 and 0.047 ns. Quartus's default
fitter effort, Auto Fit, eases off once a design meets its constraints. So
the same mapping was fitted again at 120 MHz, which no seed is near:

| Quartus 17.0.2, products in DSP blocks | 100 MHz target | 120 MHz target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seeds 1-4 | 98.67, 99.53, 105.00, 101.01 | 103.37, 98.24, 103.85, 100.19 |
| Average | 101.1 | 101.4 |
| Limited by | Q ×2, DC ×2 | Q ×4 |
| Q, DC, D, EX averages | 101.5, 103.1, 107.3, 106.2 | 101.4, 108.1, 109.3, 107.6 |
| AG, M2, WB averages | 113.2, 114.4, 125.3 | 115.0, 118.8, 125.0 |
| ALMs | 10,659-10,712 | 10,645-10,721 |

**The higher target does not raise Quartus's best.** Its best seed is
103.85, under 100 MHz's 105.00, and the average moves by 0.4 MHz, inside
the seed spread.
- **Q is the wall.** Its average is 101.4 at 120 MHz against 101.5 at 100.
  On every seed at 120 MHz, Q's worst path runs from qalign's `start` into
  D's byte register (`decode.by`), in 5 or 6 logic levels, of which 5.7-6.1
  ns is interconnect. Seed 1's critical path runs through the `dec[start]`
  stride (`Mult0_rtl_0`) and `Mux786`.
- **The other stages had headroom.** DC gains 5.0 MHz, M2 4.4, and D, AG and
  EX 1.4-2.0. At 100 MHz they were understated, but Q limits either way.

So the 100 MHz figures stand as Quartus's best for this mapping.

## The 15% comparison

nextpnr `bd7a1c0d` routed this RTL (`eeb56de165`, the mid-REP record) at
58.28-62.37 MHz, 60.5 on average, at an 80 MHz target no seed met. Its best
is 62.37 (seed 2).

| Quartus | Best seed | Over nextpnr's best |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Products in DSP blocks (the script), 100 MHz target | 105.00 (seed 3) | +68.4% |
| Products in DSP blocks, 120 MHz target | 103.85 (seed 3) | +66.5% |
| Every multiply in logic (removed) | 91.22 (seed 4) | +46.3% |

These are diagnostic figures, not the fes#1164 test itself. Best against
best on this RTL, Quartus is 68% ahead, well past the 15% line.

**Untried: fitter settings.** `OPTIMIZATION_MODE` and the physical synthesis
options (`PHYSICAL_SYNTHESIS_COMBO_LOGIC`,
`PHYSICAL_SYNTHESIS_REGISTER_RETIMING`). Quartus 17.0.2 knows these
assignments; whether Lite applies them to this device is untested. They
could raise Quartus's figure, if they shorten Q's `start` to `decode.by`
path. They are not in the script. The 120 MHz sweep, synthesis and 4
seeds, took 3.5 minutes.

## What the docs should say

This is reported for the docs' owners to write; they are unchanged here.

- **The muldiv record** names qalign's byte rotate as the 9×9 Quartus
  added. It is the `dec[start]` stride multiply (above).
- **The Quartus-cores design's i386 example** is about 70 MHz OSS against
  about 100 Quartus, about 40% more. Best against best on this RTL it is
  62.37 against 105.00, about 68% more (60.5 against 101.1 on average).
