# i386 stack: PUSH, POP, CALL rel, RET, LEAVE, JMP r/m

Dated record, 2026-10-26. Host simulation and routed diagnostics on Powerboat.
No package, RBF or kit.

After [data-port latency](2026-10-26-i386-data-latency.md) the CPU had no
stack operations. This slice adds the near ones:
- PUSH and POP of a GPR, PUSH of an immediate, and PUSH and POP of ES, CS (push
  only), SS, DS, FS and GS;
- CALL rel, RET and RET imm16, LEAVE, and JMP r/m.

CALL r/m, PUSH and POP r/m, far transfers, ENTER, PUSHA and POPA come later.

Run data: [`2026-10-26-i386-stack.jsonl`](2026-10-26-i386-stack.jsonl).
- The before figures are the data-latency rows (`68a407616c`). The routes are
  of `3c6fa64237`, and the full lockstep runs of `c3885e016b`, which changes
  only the harness since.
- The one RTL change after that makes the package's `STK_*` values an enum,
  for Verilator's lint. It gives the same netlist size, labels and depth, and
  the same retirements on a shorter lockstep run.
- A Quartus row's `fingerprint` is the first 12 hex digits of the SHA-256 of
  its `sources` map, as JSON with sorted keys.

## The data path

- **One uop each, with a memory operand at SS:ESP** (SS:EBP for LEAVE), at
  the stack's width (SS.B). A push stores at ESP less the size, a pop loads at
  ESP.
- **ESP is a second destination.**
  - AG has a third adder for the new ESP: ESP less the size for a push, plus
    it for a pop, plus imm16 for RET imm16, EBP plus it for LEAVE.
  - WB writes it beside the main destination. The main destination wins when
    it is ESP too (POP ESP).
  - D's tables (`fes_i386_dops`, `fes_i386_dfwd`) follow it as an early result
    from AG. So a push or pop right after another takes its address with no
    wait.
- **CALL rel is two uops.**
  - The first pushes the next EIP. It carries the displacement in its
    immediate, which a push does not use.
  - D builds the second, JMP rel, as it issues it. A step bit keeps the
    instruction in `dc` meanwhile.
- **RET and JMP r/m** take their target from EX's adder (CS's base plus the
  EIP the uop loaded or read). They redirect from WB like any taken branch.
- **Two waits in D:**
  - ESP read as data waits while a stack operation is in AG. Its value reaches
    the M1 register next cycle.
  - LEAVE on a 16-bit stack waits until ESP's high half, which it keeps, is in
    a register.

### Keeping D's depth

The first build cost D a LUT level, 8 to 9. The CALL step selected a whole
second uop ahead of D's register and address lookups.

What took D back to 8:
- **Register fields from the first uop.** D's lookups take the first uop's
  register fields, since neither of CALL's uops names a register. The step bit
  gates only whether the address is used.
- **Stack base chosen in DC.** DC picks the base, ESP or EBP, so D's lookup
  starts from `dc`'s register.
- **One early bit for ESP.** ESP from a stack operation in AG is a single
  early bit beside the destination's, not an early bit per register.
- **No second uop in `dc`.** DC stores only the displacement, in the push's
  immediate, which saves 126 flip-flops. CALL shares JMP rel's immediate
  decode, which kept DC at 10 levels.

## What the 386EX does

Corpus and oracle agree on all of these:
- **A 32-bit PUSH or POP of a segment register** moves ESP by 4 but transfers
  only the selector's word.
  - POP GS at ESP FFFEh does not fault, as a 4-byte read would.
  - PUSH writes 2 bytes.
- **In 16-bit code, a RET or JMP r/m whose target is past FFFFh** raises
  #GP(0) at the branch, with nothing popped.
  - In 101 of 2,000 corpus states the dword a 32-bit RET pops lies past
    FFFFh.
  - The RTL raises it in EX, beside the target, under CS's real-mode limit.
  - Under a larger limit it is unimplemented, as for the fetch check.

**nextpnr averages 73.2 MHz against 71.0.** That is within the spread of the
data-latency seeds.
- Q limits seeds 1 and 3 on its own loop (`align.linear_here` → `issue` →
  `acc`, 7 cells, 76-77% routing), as before. Q's RTL is unchanged.
- Seed 2 is D's address lookup (`dfwd.ready` → `base_value` → `ag_row`, 8
  cells).
- Seed 4 is DC's load (`dc` → `dfwd.ag_early`/`late` → `advance` → `dc`, 7
  cells).

**Quartus averages 100.8 MHz against 103.3, every seed Q-limited.**
- Seed 4's 96.5 is the lowest Q has had.
- Q's RTL is unchanged, so this is the fitter's placement of a design that is
  10% larger.

**Yosys's depth matches the data-latency slice in every stage:** D 8, DC 10,
EX 10, M1 7, M2 7, WB 6. Q, whose RTL is unchanged, gives 8 or 9 from one ABC
run to the next. AG is 5 LUT levels plus 29 carry cells against 18; the third
adder runs beside the others.

**The corpus.** It gains 80,500 states: every form of the stack stems,
operand-size forms included.

**Random programs** now also take:
- pushes and pops of GPRs, immediates and segment registers;
- CALLs to subroutines that return with RET, or RET imm16 at the outer
  level, up to two deep;
- PUSH BP / MOV BP, SP / LEAVE frames whose bodies may push.

Constraints that keep each return address valid:
- A subroutine's body writes neither ESP, SS nor memory, and pushes and pops
  in pairs of one size.
- No backward jump lands inside a subroutine.
- A frame's body is unconstrained: it may write BP, SP or memory before
  LEAVE. Both sides agree on whatever LEAVE then does, but such frames test
  less of it.

There are 15.8M random retirements against 18.2M before, because fewer
programs run to the 2,000-retirement limit.

**The holds stay cycle-exact,** and a new one is checked:
- **Push or pop after another: one cycle.** All 1,906 such retirements in the
  latency-free reruns are one cycle behind the one before.
- **Checked elsewhere:** a pop right after a push is a load after a store, at
  least two cycles. A push or pop just after a segment load waits for the
  base.
- **Left out of that check:**
  - CALL, which is two uops;
  - LEAVE;
  - PUSH and POP of (E)SP, which read ESP as data;
  - anything after POP (E)SP, which loads ESP.
- **Other instructions after a push or pop:** 87,461, of which 7.5% wait a
  cycle for ESP.

Mutations, each on 3,500 corpus states (seven stack stems) and 2 × 200
random programs:

| Mutation | Result |
| --- | --- |
| D does not wait for ESP data behind a stack operation in AG | 229 of 400 programs fail; the latency-free trace changes |
| D's data-operand table ignores ESP from stack operations | 238 of 400 programs fail; the latency-free trace changes |
| D's address table ignores ESP from stack operations | 260 of 400 programs fail; the latency-free trace changes |

The corpus, one instruction per run, passes each mutation.

**Harness faults found on the way, each fixed:**
- **Backward jumps into subroutines.** A backward jump could land inside a
  subroutine and run its RET with no CALL.
- **Nested RET imm16.** A nested subroutine's RET imm16 moved ESP past the
  outer return address.

## Review fault regressions

The lockstep runner now includes three directed Bochs cases:

| Case | Input | Required result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Relative CALL | `66 E8 00 00 01 00` at EIP 0, 16-bit CS limit FFFFh | #GP(0), with EIP, ESP and memory unchanged; the push does not commit |
| Near RET | `66 C3` pops 00010000h from SS:ESP | #GP(0), with EIP, ESP and memory unchanged |
| Indirect JMP | `66 FF E3` with EBX=00010000h | #GP(0), with EIP, ESP and memory unchanged |

The RET and JMP cases have a younger `MOV AX,[SI]` after the branch. After
each fault the test clocks 32 cycles and checks the fetch request, D issue
trace and data-read output; all remain inactive. The CALL target is checked
on the first uop, which carries the fault instead of committing the push, so
an out-of-limit target faults without changing ESP or memory.

The updated design document describes the third AG adder, the one-cycle
ESP-as-data stall, the 16-bit LEAVE wait and CALL's two issue cycles. The
ESP-forwarding comment now identifies the M1 table entry that records the AG
producer after D's stall.
