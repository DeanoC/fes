# i386 EX cut: shift flags off EX, shifter decoded in M2

Dated record, 2026-10-25. Host simulation and routed diagnostics on Powerboat.
No package, RBF or kit.

The [Quartus oracle record](2026-10-25-i386-quartus-oracle.md) found EX
limiting the whole CPU on both flows. Its worst path ran from EX's registered
controls through the shifter and the result select into the flags. This cut
takes that path apart. Both flows were re-routed, seeds 1-4:
- nextpnr `bd7a1c0d` through `gpu-run`, HIP, with `NEXTPNR_REQUIRE_GPU=1` and
  an 80 MHz target;
- Quartus 17.0.2 through `scripts/quartus_fes_i386.py --dsp-in-logic` at
  100 MHz.

Run data: [`2026-10-25-i386-ex-cut.jsonl`](2026-10-25-i386-ex-cut.jsonl).

The RTL carries the DeanoC/yosys#59 workaround, the whole-record
`at_start` copy in qalign. DeanoC/yosys#60, which fixes #59, has not merged
into the lock. The RTL README lists the copy among the rules.

- **EX stops limiting on both flows.**
  - nextpnr: EX rises from 56.9-61.9 to 66.6-70.0 MHz. D is now the limit at
    62.6-64.8, where it already was before.
  - Quartus: EX rises from 86.8-88.8 to 97.2-105.5 MHz. D, Q and EX now sit
    within 4 MHz of each other, at 96-105.
- **The cost is one cycle** when an instruction that consumes the flags comes
  directly after a shift or rotate. Nothing else changes in timing.

## The cut

Before, the worst EX paths ran (Quartus, 10-11 levels):
1. the operand extract;
2. four levels of the shifter's opcode- and size-dependent arrangement;
3. the shift;
4. `r_other`;
5. the ZF/SF/PF reduction;
6. `ex_flags`.

The result path to `wb_s` was 0.2-0.8 ns behind it.

1. **Shift flags leave EX's flag path.**
   - For a shift or rotate, EX hands WB its result, the shifter's raw CF and
     OF, and two bits: CF/OF come from the shifter, and SF/ZF/PF follow the
     result (AF is set).
   - The flags take effect a cycle later, from the WB register. ZF, SF and PF
     are reduced from `wb_s.result` by size and byte lane.
   - That patched copy feeds every uop's passthrough flags, refills EX's
     running `ex_flags` every cycle, and is what WB retires to EFLAGS.
2. **D holds a flag consumer one cycle behind a shift or rotate.**
   - The consumers are ADC, SBB, Jcc, SETcc, RCL, RCR, LAHF, CMC, LOOPE and
     LOOPNE.
   - Such a uop must not read `ex_flags` in the cycle it still lacks the
     shift's flags.
   - The test reads the opcode and ModRM bytes directly (`decode.consumes`),
     not the decode's outputs, so it stays off D's issue path.
   - This is a hold at D, like the LEA and selector stalls. No stage behind D
     holds, so D's forwarding tables need no new check.
3. **The shifter's controls are decoded in M2.** `exctl` registers one-hot
   selects for direction × size × fill (operand, rotate, rotate through
   carry, sign, SHLD/SHRD source), the immediate shift amount, and the CF
   cases. Each bit of the arrangement is an OR of gated data bits, with no
   opcode compare in front of the shift.
   - The old `shifted ? r_shift : an` select is gone: the funnel already
     returns `a` at a count of zero.
4. **A logic op's SF, ZF and PF come from the logic unit.** They no longer
   come from the whole result select, which also carried SETcc's condition,
   LAHF, the extends and LEA.

The intermediate commit `2db051f410` had (1), (3) and (4), with the hold
reading the decode's flag reads. It routed at 56.9-67.1 MHz on nextpnr, D
limiting, and 98.9-103.6 MHz on Quartus. Moving the consumer test to the raw
bytes (`7b771a8444`) changed neither flow beyond the seed spread. The jsonl
holds both.

## Cost

In the latency-free random reruns (seeds 1 and 2, 2,000 programs each):
- 99,743 flag consumers came directly after a shift. Every one retired two
  cycles after it; before this cut it would have been one.
- 72,909 other instructions after a shift retired one cycle after it, as
  before.
- Sequential instructions stayed 100% one cycle apart.

There is no new forwarding case. A shift's result forwards as before, and only
its flags wait a cycle.

## Rule 8

- **The flag path is fixed.** EX's limiting paths on both flows are now
  shifter-to-result paths with no carry chain: operand, arrangement, two shift
  levels, the result select, then the register. That is 7-8 levels in Quartus,
  and no flag reduction follows the shifter any more.
- **The adder half of rule 8 is not met.** This cut left the adder alone.
  - With EX synthesized alone, register to register, Yosys maps the
    adder-derived CF and OF at 8 LUT levels around a 32-33-cell carry chain,
    against rule 8's three (two before, one after). ABC's level counts are
    noisy, as D's showed.
  - In the RTL, the bypass, byte extract and operand preparation sit before
    the chain. The size's carry select and the flag or result select sit
    after it.
  - Those paths do not limit on either flow today.

## Next limits

**nextpnr: D, 62.6-64.8 MHz.**
- The path: the skid register (`skid_valid` / `skid`), then `cur`, the decode,
  `d_out`, and from there `ag_row`, `ag_s`, `d_stop` and `dfwd.ag_early`.
- The routed paths carry 9-11 cells, 65-72% of it routing.
- It is decode feeding D's operand and issue registers. The skid mux in front
  of decode is one of its levels.
- Next behind it are EX at 66.6-70.0 and Q at 70.8-76.8.

**Quartus: D, Q and EX together, 96-105 MHz.**
- D: decode into the register indices and the operand forwarding select (for
  example `skid_valid` → `decode.s2_idx` → the operand mux), at 7-8 levels and
  60-79% interconnect.
- Q: the 32:1 select of `align.q` from `start`, at 5-6 levels of muxes.
- EX: the shifter-to-result path above.

## What the design doc should say about the clock

This is reported for the doc's owner to write; the doc is unchanged here.

- **Quartus.** The whole CPU closes at 96-101 MHz. 100 MHz needs D and Q cut
  alongside EX, since all three sit within a few MHz of each other.
- **nextpnr.** The OSS flow closes at 62.6-64.8 MHz with D limiting, behind
  the place-and-route gap DeanoC/nextpnr#300 records. Under rule 9 the clock
  lands at the measured figure. D is the next cut.
- **Latency table.** It should gain a row: a flag consumer right after a shift
  or rotate costs 1 extra cycle.
- **Early branches and the early ALU.** Any later flag reader ahead of WB,
  such as a Jcc resolved at AG or an early ADC or SBB, must either read the
  WB-patched flags or take the same one-cycle wait behind a shift.
