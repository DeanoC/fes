# i386 multiply and divide: MUL, IMUL, DIV, IDIV, AAM and AAD

Dated record, 2026-10-27. Host simulation and routed diagnostics on Powerboat.
No package, RBF or kit.

After [the issue cut](2026-10-27-i386-issue.md), EX gains a multiply and
divide unit (`fes_i386_muldiv`) for:
- MUL and IMUL r/m (F6h/F7h /4, /5), IMUL r, r/m (0F AFh) and IMUL r, r/m,
  imm (69h, 6Bh);
- DIV and IDIV (F6h/F7h /6, /7), which raise #DE (vector 0) for a zero
  divisor or an overflowing quotient;
- AAM and AAD (D4h, D5h), AAM 0 raising #DE.

Their flags follow the 386EX as SingleStepTests/80386 records them, the
undefined ones and the FLAGS a divide error pushes included. BOUND, the BCD
adjusts, the bit tests and BSF/BSR stay out.

Run data: [`2026-10-27-i386-muldiv.jsonl`](2026-10-27-i386-muldiv.jsonl).
- The before figures are the issue cut's (`36a3567502`, its record's rows).
- Routes, Quartus fits, the full lockstep runs and the mutations are of

- The rows also hold two earlier commits' routes and fits, and two

## The 386EX's flags

The 386 manual leaves SF, ZF, AF and PF undefined after MUL and IMUL, all six
arithmetic flags after DIV and IDIV, and CF, OF and AF after AAM and AAD.
The 386EX's values follow from its microcode's algorithms, worked out here
from the corpus. Every rule below matches every state of its 38 files that
does not fault for another reason (#UD or #GP), 87,212 in all:

| Instruction | The 386EX | States |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MUL, IMUL (all forms) | The flags of the last step of a shift-add multiply, right to left, that stops once the multiplier's remaining bits are all zeros (all ones for a negative IMUL multiplier), after at least 3 steps | 53,897 |
| DIV | The flags of the last step's trial subtract of the divisor from the shifted partial remainder, at the operand size | 13,907 |
| DIV raising #DE | The pushed FLAGS: the first compare's subtract kept, then one step fewer | 432 |
| IDIV, with or without #DE | The flags of the remainder's sign fix, after dividing a negative dividend's one's complement | 14,104 |
| AAM | AL plus 0: SF, ZF and PF of AL, CF, OF and AF clear; AAM 0's divide error pushes the divide's | 2,436 |
| AAD | The 8-bit ADD of AH × imm to AL (as the divergence list had it) | 2,436 |

- **MUL and IMUL.** With a the multiplicand and m the multiplier (r/m; imm
  for 69h and 6Bh), the multiply takes k = max(bits(m), 3) steps for m ≥ 0.
  The flags are those of the last step's ADD of a to the partial product,
  even where m's bit there is clear. That sum is P >> (k − 1) for the whole
  product P, or (P >> 2) + a when m < 4, so the RTL takes it from the DSP
  product with a shift. For a negative IMUL multiplier the step count is
  k = min(n, max(bits(~m), trailing zeros(m) + 4)), and the step is a SUB
  of a. Its difference is P >> (k − 1) when m's bit k − 1 is clear, else
  that less a, and −a for m = −2^(n−1).
  - The cycle counts in the corpus's bus traces agree: a MUL by m takes 24 +
    max(bits(m), 3) cycles to its HLT; a negative IMUL multiplier's count
    follows bits(~m) with the same floor, rising with trailing zeros.
- **DIV.** A restoring divide, a bit per step, over n steps; the bit
  shifted out of the partial remainder's top counts as a quotient bit. The
  flags are its last trial subtract's (CF its borrow).
  - The bus traces show a divide error comes after the whole loop: the IVT
    read follows the instruction by 35, 43 and 60 cycles for 8-, 16- and
    32-bit DIV, and 42, 50 and 67 for IDIV, whatever the operands.
  - For DIV the error flags are the first compare's subtract, kept (the
    dividend's high half is not below the divisor), then n − 1 steps.
  - With a zero divisor that gives the flags of the dividend shifted right
    by one, at the operand size; AAM 0 is that for AL.
- **IDIV.** The 386EX divides a negative dividend's one's complement by the
  divisor's magnitude, with no top bit out, so the remainder is one more:
  a remainder equal to the divisor becomes zero and the quotient one more.
  - It checks the quotient for its sign's range after that fix, as Bochs
    does. No corpus state separates this from checking before it.
  - On an overflowing dividend the steps leave a garbage quotient. Where it
    passes the check (80h for an 8-bit divide with the signs differing) the
    instruction completes without #DE. These are the 9 cases the divergence
    list had named without a rule; they are now a predicate.
  - The flags, the #DE frame's too, are those of the remainder's sign fix:
    x = the remainder, or its complement for a negative dividend, then the
    divisor subtracted from x when dividend and divisor have the same sign,
    else added.

## The unit

`fes_i386_muldiv` works in EX while D to WB freeze, from EX's operands,
which stay put meanwhile. Only registers lie between it and EX. In its
first cycle it makes its operands from what EX makes them of, bypassed and
at the uop's size, and registers them with the uop's decode, so the DSPs
start from registers. It registers its result by
the cycle before its last, then writes it into the uop's record (`ex_s`),
and hands EX its flags from a register. The uop leaves on the cycle after
the unit's last, with its result, merged into the destination's old value,
and its flags.

| Form | Uops | Cycles the unit works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MUL, IMUL r/m8, IMUL r, r/m (imm) | 1 | 5 |
| MUL, IMUL r/m16/32 | MUL into EAX, then HI: the kept high half into EDX (`SEQ_MUL`) | 5 |
| DIV, IDIV r/m8 | 1 | 12, 13 |
| DIV, IDIV r/m16/32 | LOAD: EDX into the unit (it reads the memory operand too, so a fault comes first); the divide into EAX; HI: the remainder into EDX (`SEQ_DIV`) | 20 and 36; 21 and 37 |
| AAM, AAD | 1 | 12; 5 |

- **Multiply.** The operands, registered (E0), four 18×18 signed DSP
  products of the 33-bit operands (E1), their sum as one 3:2 row and one
  carry chain (E2), the result halves, CF and OF (E3), and the undefined
  flags from the product shifted by the 386EX's step count, which E2 works
  out from the multiplier registered at E0 (E4).
- **Divide.** Restoring, a bit per cycle, with the flags of each step's
  trial subtract kept: the operands, the dividend's load, DIV's first
  compare, then n steps (n − 1 after an overflow), then a cycle for the
  result. Each step also registers the result it would leave. IDIV's
  magnitudes take the compare's cycle, and its sign fixes two cycles after
  its n steps: the one more and the quotient's sign are one adder, and a
  negative dividend's remainder is the complement of the remainder before
  its one more.
- **Through EX's registers.** The result enters the uop's record and leaves
  through LEA's select; the high half a HI uop writes enters its record as
  it leaves M2, and EX's lanes merge it into EDX. #DE is the record's fault
  (`FAULT_DE`), set in the unit's last cycle, and WB delivers it as vector
  0, beside the other faults, so the delivery pushes the divide's flags.
- **The wait.** `md_busy` is a register set as the uop enters EX, which
  clears in the unit's last cycle; `freeze` gains it as one more registered
  input, and D's issue no new term. A flush waiting in WB cuts the wait
  after a cycle, as the uop behind it is on the wrong path. The uop in WB
  waits too, so its retirement shows the wait; the lockstep counts such
  gaps apart (`trace_md_wait`).

## What the design doc should say

This is reported for the doc's owner to write; the doc is unchanged here.

- **Multiplier.** MUL and IMUL take four DSP blocks and 5 cycles in EX while
  D to WB freeze, every uop behind waiting, rather than three pipelined
  cycles where only dependent uops wait. The unit's first cycle registers
  its operands, so nothing of EX's bypass reaches the DSPs, and the
  undefined flags need the product shifted by the 386EX's step count, a
  cycle more.
- **Divider.** Restoring, a bit per cycle without leading-zero skip, as the
  386EX's: 36 cycles for DIV r/m32, the 386EX's flags falling out of the
  steps. #DE is raised at EX with the flags the divide changed.
- **AAM and AAD** use the divider and the multiplier, AAM 0 raising #DE.
- **The 386EX's undefined flags** for these instructions follow its
  microcode's loops, as above, and IDIV lets some overflows through.
- **EX's area budget holds; the clock does not.** The unit is about 950
  ALMs and 4 DSP blocks on Quartus, inside EX's 2,500-ALM line. But on
  nextpnr the CPU loses about 6 MHz with it, by placement: EX sits at the
  edge of the CPU's region, and the unit's 2,500 cells beside it stretch EX
  and D. Levers the doc could weigh:
  - a floorplan region for EX and the unit;
  - a smaller multiplier: the 386EX's undefined flags alone take about 400
    cells (the step count, and the product shifted by it);
  - an iterative multiply on the divider's adder, which gives the 386EX's
    flags directly but takes up to n cycles.
- **The DSPs start from registers.** nextpnr places them 20 to 30 columns
  from EX, so their inputs are a cycle of their own.
- **Quartus with DSPs** also maps `qalign`'s byte rotate to a DSP block.
