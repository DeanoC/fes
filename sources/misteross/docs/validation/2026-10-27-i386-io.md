# i386 I/O: IN, OUT, INS and OUTS

Dated record, 2026-10-27. Host simulation and routed diagnostics on Powerboat.
No package, RBF or kit.

This slice builds the real-mode I/O instructions on an I/O port, as the
design's [I/O](../i386-cpu-design.md#io) and
[machine interface](../i386-cpu-design.md#machine-interface) sections ask:
- IN and OUT of a byte, word or dword, with an imm8 or DX port (E4h-E7h,
  ECh-EFh);
- INS and OUTS (6Ch-6Fh), with REP, on the string engine.

Run data: [`2026-10-27-i386-io.jsonl`](2026-10-27-i386-io.jsonl).
- Everything here is of `5ca51f93b3`.
- The before figures are a route of `535a05a4dd`, the string waits slice's
  branch now, with MUL/DIV (#1167) merged in. That record's own figures
  (nextpnr 66.9, Quartus 104.3) are from before the merge, so they are not
  the before here.
- Eight nextpnr seeds. A Quartus row's `fingerprint` is the first 12 hex
  digits of the SHA-256 of its `sources` map, as JSON with sorted keys.

## The port

| Port | Direction | Contract |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `io_req` | Out | The uop in WB makes an access, until the machine completes it |
| `io_write`, `io_port`, `io_size`, `io_wdata` | Out | OUT or IN, the port, a byte, word or dword (0, 1, 2), OUT's data; held while `io_req` is high |
| `io_ack`, `io_rdata` | In | The access is complete on this edge, with IN's data |

A request after an ack is the next access.

## How it runs

- **At WB, never speculatively.** The uop in WB raises `io_req`, and D to WB
  freeze until `io_ack`. The uop retires on the edge after.
  - Whether WB holds an I/O uop (`io_wb`) is a register set as it enters
    WB, and so is the ack (`io_done`). So the freeze has two more registered
    inputs, and D's issue none from the port.
  - A uop that enters WB with a fault makes no access. INS's store and
    OUTS's load check their segment first, as on the 386.
  - There is no write buffer yet: every older store has written when the
    access is made.
- **The data.**
  - IN merges the port's data into eAX by size, at WB.
  - INS stores it at ES:EDI as a STOS element would.
  - OUTS loads seg:ESI as LODS does and sends the loaded value. OUT sends
    eAX.
- **What follows waits.** Nothing younger issues until an I/O instruction
  has retired (`io_busy`, kept as `t_busy` is). So no load reads memory an
  OUT may remap, and no uop reads IN's data before the register file holds
  it. A REP INS or OUTS's own elements still issue back to back, each
  waiting in WB for its access. The next instruction's first uop waits.
- **The port and data registers** are read at WB, from the uop's record. D
  forwards them as it does any operand, and EX takes one whose producer, one
  stage ahead, has just computed it from the WB register into the record.
  So an OUT right after MOV DX, imm issues with no wait.
- **Real mode** has no I/O permission check. The TSS bitmap check comes with
  protected mode.

**Rates, without latency**, with the machine acknowledging in the cycle the
request rises:

| Run | Cycles |
| --- | --- |
| REP INS or OUTS, any size | 2 an element |
| IN or OUT, then HLT | 6 more than NOP, HLT |
| IN AL, DX twice | 13 more |

An I/O instruction costs one cycle of its own in WB. The rest is the next
instruction's wait to issue, then its way through D to WB.

## The testbench

- **Two I/O spaces.** The oracle and the RTL each have one (`IoSpace`).
  - In corpus mode every port reads all ones and ignores writes, as the
    corpus's test bus did. Bochs reads all ones too, so the
    `386ex-port-22h` entry still follows the oracle.
  - In random programs each port holds a byte. An OUT writes it; an IN reads
    it, then increments it. A word or dword access covers the next ports,
    wrapping at 64K.
- **Each instruction's accesses** are compared with the oracle's, in order:
  write or read, port, length and data, as memory writes are. Not yet in a
  double fault: that check returns before the I/O compare, so a REP INS or
  OUTS whose fault's delivery faults again has its accesses before the fault
  unchecked.
- **The RTL's port** completes 0-12 cycles after a request rises, at random,
  as the fetch and data ports do. Until then `io_rdata` is garbage, and the
  request must hold its port, size and data.
- **The random programs** gain an I/O block:
  - one to three IN and OUT of every size and form, on a few ports (DX =
    FFFFh among them, whose word wraps), now and then just after DX, AH or
    eAX is written;
  - or a REP INS or OUTS (or one without REP) of up to four elements, with
    ES = DS, so OUTS can read what INS stored.
- **The runner** adds the twelve I/O opcodes to `SCOPE`, and IN and OUT
  leave the unbuilt list. The scope test's unbuilt example is now CLTS
  (0F06h).

**The slice costs nothing measurable on either flow.** nextpnr averages 64.4
MHz against 61.7, Quartus 87.0 against 87.6. Every stage moves within its
seeds' spread.

- **Quartus is limited by the multiplier now.** Every seed's worst path, on
  both sides, is `muldiv`'s soft 18×18 products in EX. The MUL/DIV record
  found the same with DSPs in logic (89.7). That is the stack's Quartus
  limit since MUL/DIV, not this slice's.
- **On nextpnr** the before is EX-limited on six seeds: the unit's placement,
  as the MUL/DIV record found. Here DC's decode limits five.
- **The two `envelope` seeds are D's paths.** They end at the string
  engine's count predicates and first-element flags (`r_c2`, `s_kill1`).
  The benchmark's D pattern has missed those registers since #1170, so they
  fall to `envelope`. The rows keep the labels they were routed with.
- **The new registers land where they are labelled:** `io_busy` in D,
  `io_wb` in EX, `io_done` and `io_data` in WB. `envelope` grows by the
  spike's `io_ack` and `io_rdata` only, 33 flip-flops.

## What the design doc should say

This is reported for the doc's owner to write; the doc is unchanged here.

- **An I/O instruction drains what follows:** nothing younger issues until
  it retires, but a REP INS or OUTS's own elements. That keeps loads from
  passing an OUT that remaps memory, and IN's data off the forwarding paths.
- **The machine interface's I/O row** is the port above: a request held
  until an ack, with IN's data on the ack's edge.
- **The DSP policy under fes#1164.** With DSPs in logic, the multiplier
  limits Quartus (87.6 MHz on the stack now). With DSPs, Q limits, through
  `qalign`'s rotate (the MUL/DIV record's 85.3). The i386's Quartus variant
  needs one of them chosen, or the rotate kept out of the DSPs.
