# #1861 / PR #1862: netlist equivalence for the GPU-router selection

PR #1862 edits five producer scripts and one shared helper
(`scripts/fes_de10nano_evidence.py`). No RTL, QSF, SDC, lock or placement
recipe changes. The record of every artefact whose producer closure holds a
changed file therefore moves through its source inputs, and with it `BUILD_ID`,
so [D12](../../../AGENTS.md) applies.

**This record was re-derived at the merge base `a482f14c0` after three things
moved underneath it, and main has moved under it three more times since.** This
page numbers the base moves one to four, counting the move onto `a482f14c0`
itself as the first, and the `reports-scope`, `-scope2`, `-scope3` and
`-scope4` directories hold them in that order: `72d2fc4da` (the PR's first
merge base) -> `a482f14c0` -> `e2b8130b5` -> `5e592f003` -> `6ee9f1113`, which
is the sequence in the Trees table. The earlier version of this page claimed a five-artefact
scope agreed verbally on 2026-11-15; that claim is withdrawn below, with what
replaced it and why.

## Where this stands

D12 is discharged for every affected artefact. The table under "Results" is
generated by `d12_status.py` from the three summarisers' JSON, so it cannot
drift from the evidence; these are the same facts in prose.

- **Condition 1: 15 of 15.** Eleven Yosys netlists byte-identical on two
  agreeing passes, both sides, plus the four ZX81-family canonical QSFs, which
  is the object D12 names for a native Quartus front end. `splash` is the one
  artefact needing the `src` path exemption. Every row's pinned inputs differ
  between the sides while its netlist does not, and no row passed vacuously.
- **Condition 2: 11 of 11 full-tier.** Seven measured at this base (three
  nextpnr, four Quartus Lite), three carried from `72d2fc4da` under the
  merge-forward clause, and `splash` on its own stronger footing. The four
  `reduced` artefacts are not owed it (#1863).
- **Condition 3: 11 of 11 full-tier,** across two heads and tied to them by a
  named anchor, because the producers record the revision three different ways
  or not at all: `revision` (the Quartus lane), `source_commit` (`fes.menu`), a
  BUILD_ID re-derived at the head (`splash`), and the full set of input digests
  (`fes.c64`, 73 files; the Spectrum family, 52 each). No row is unverified.
  Each Quartus model's repeat run reproduced its sign-off RBF.

Condition 3 spans two heads because of the first two of those moves.
The four Spectrum machines were resealed at `541e3c548751`, since #1948 edited
their producer and condition 3 never carries across an identity move. The other
seven were taken at `df99f2dfb208`, and their identities are *unchanged* at
`541e3c548751` -- measured in `reports-scope2/cond3-head-move.log`, which reports
only `fes.ramtest`, the Spectrum four and the out-of-scope `fes.pcxt-dos` as
moved.

The third and fourth moves merged main again, so neither of those two heads is
this branch's tip any more. Rather than argue from the commit graph that the seals
survived it, `seals_at_head.py` re-checks each one against the files on disk
now: every digest in the seal's own inputs map against the file at that path,
and, for the four Quartus summaries that carry no inputs map, a design-identity
comparison between the tree the seal was taken in and this one. All eleven are
intact at the current tip (`reports-scope4/seals-at-head.log`; the run at the
third base is kept at `reports-scope3/seals-at-head.log`). That check is
anchored on the
`sources/misteross` tree object, not on a commit SHA: a commit SHA moves every
time this record's own documents are committed -- the commit storing the result
included -- so citing one would leave the statement stale on arrival for a
reason that has nothing to do with what was measured.

**One margin is thin and is called out deliberately.**
`fes.spectrum-plus3`'s system clock closes at 52.768 MHz against a 52.225 MHz
constraint, 1.0104x, where the next thinnest is `fes.c64` at 11%. It passes,
and nothing was tuned or waived. But at one per cent the identity constant
alone can decide the next build, so a future miss there is not evidence of
whatever change is in flight at the time. See "A thin margin" below.

## After the merge: the evidence this record opens, and the SHAs a squash leaves behind

PR #1862 merged as `2393a9f04` on 2026-11-18 and the campaign's 72 GB of
scratch was deleted. Two things in this record pointed at that scratch, and
they are not the same kind of thing.

**Most of the `/data/fes1861-netlist/...` paths in these reports are labels.**
They say which tree a measurement was taken in, next to the revision and the
pinned-input digest that actually pin it. They are provenance, they were never
re-read, and deleting the tree does not weaken them.

**One was not a label.** Each row of `reports-cond3/cond3.json` named its
build summary by absolute path, and `seals_at_head.py` *opens* that file: the
strongest check here re-hashes every path in the summary's `inputs` map against
the tree under review. Those eleven summaries are also the one artefact of this
campaign that a rerun need not reproduce, being sign-off output of hours of
Quartus and nextpnr whose achieved frequencies move with identity alone --
by 9.4% on identical RTL, as the thin-margin section measures. So they were
copied out before the delete and are now vendored into the record at
`reports-cond3/summaries/<artefact>/build-summary.json`, with `source` naming
the copy relative to `cond3.json` and `source_sha256` pinning its bytes.
`vendor_cond3.py` does that, and re-verifies with `--check`.

What the vendoring is worth, measured rather than asserted, in
`reports-preserve/counterexamples.log`:

| Perturbation | Result |
| --- | --- |
| control | 11 of 11, rc=0 |
| a vendored summary reformatted after the fact | `SUBSTITUTED`, rc=1 |
| the same, on a row that also has a design anchor | `SUBSTITUTED`, rc=1 -- an edited seal is not rescued |
| `source_sha256` dropped from every row | `unpinned` on all eleven, rc=1 |
| every row pointed back into the deleted scratch | four rows `unverifiable`, 7 of 11, rc=1 |
| one vendored summary deleted | `ABSENT`, rc=1 |
| one artefact's summary filed under another's row, pin updated to match | `WRONG SEAL` on BUILD_ID, rc=1 |
| `vendor_cond3.py --check` on the edited copy | names the row, rc=1 |
| `vendor_cond3.py` re-run on an already-vendored collection | 11 of 11, rc=0, `cond3.json` unchanged |

The `unvendored` row is the honest size of the problem this change fixes.
Without the vendored summaries the four `541e3c548`-sealed Spectrum-family rows
cannot be verified at all, because the design anchor that carries the ZX81 rows
is a comparison from `df99f2dfb` and is refused for a row sealed in a different
tree. The other seven degrade instead to that second anchor, which is a weaker
statement than their own input digests but still a statement about condition 3.
So: four rows lost outright, seven demoted.

The `ABSENT` row reads that way only because this change made it so. Deleting a
vendored summary used to leave the row "unverifiable", which handed it to the
design anchor and reported 11 of 11 with the file gone -- so "verifies from a
clone" would have survived the vendored files being deleted again, which is the
one failure this is here to prevent. A row whose `source` is still an absolute
path into a build tree keeps the old fallback, which is why the `unvendored`
case above still reads 7 of 11 rather than 0.

### What the re-verification is pinned to, and when it expires

`_usable_routes` accepts a route comparison only if it ends at the checkout's
own `sources/misteross` and `config` trees, which is what stops a comparison
from another head being described as applying to this one. So the reports in
`reports-preserve/` are pinned to exactly one pair:

```

config            b4bb5aa7145110d9dabd482097080c608e240c99   = main at 1ec592670
```

Main's sources tree moved within hours of #1862 merging and will move again.
When it does, running this against a *current* clone of main reads 0 of 11 with
both comparisons ignored -- and that is the refusal working, not a defect. The
durable form names the commit the evidence is about:

```
$ git worktree add --detach /tmp/pinned 1ec592670
$ python3 seals_at_head.py --cond3 reports-cond3/cond3.json --tree /tmp/pinned \
    --design-move reports-preserve/seal-to-head-design.json \
```

`reports-preserve/pinned-head.log` is that run. Re-anchoring the record at a
later head is cheap -- no GPU, no Quartus -- but it needs *checkouts of the two
seal commits*, which is the strongest reason to keep them reachable: without
them the eleven seals can never be re-tied to a newer main, only to this one.

### The squash merge, measured

`#1862` was squash-merged, so no commit of that branch is an ancestor of main.
`check_shas.py` asks only whether a cited object resolves *now*, which it does
only while some ref still holds it. `sha_exposure.py` asks the sharper
question, and `reports-preserve/sha-exposure.log` has the counts; the shape of
the answer is two groups rather than one number:

- **Fourteen commits from `#1862`'s branch**, both condition-3 seal commits
  (`df99f2dfb`, `541e3c548`) among them, and with them three tree objects. Deleting the merged branch -- routine
  housekeeping -- would make `check_shas.py` fail and strip the provenance from
  all fourteen. One annotated tag at `e3fc6c144fac` covers every one of them.
  It is not pushed here: pushing a tag to the shared repository is Deano's call.
- **This PR's own commits**, which the reports under `reports-preserve/` cite
  as the head they were measured at. Merging this PR with a merge commit makes
  them ancestors of main; squashing it repeats the trap one more time.

`sha_exposure.py` prints "17 cited commits are NOT ancestors of origin/main"
at this checkout, and the clone proof above prints "17 objects ... do not
resolve". **Those are two different seventeens, and neither is the other.** The
clone's seventeen is #1862's branch alone -- fourteen commits plus three trees
-- because a clone of *this* branch holds this PR's commits. The checkout's
seventeen is those same fourteen commits plus three of this PR's own, and it
skips trees entirely, because a tree is held by whatever commit holds it. The
figure to act on is the clone's: seventeen objects lost if #1862's branch is
deleted. And what a *reader* needs is narrower than what is cited --
the anchors these checks compare are the `sources/misteross` and `config` tree
objects, and those are main's.

## Changes on main that moved the work, and what each did

**#1891 moved the ZX81 family to a Quartus front end.** `fes.zx81`, `fes.zx80`,
`fes.ts1000` and `fes.ts1500` now select `build_fes_zx81_quartus`, whose own
`SOURCE_ROOTS` name `scripts/fes_de10nano_evidence.py`, so they remain affected
artefacts. What changed is the object D12 compares: that producer runs no Yosys
program, and D12 says "for a native Quartus front end, its QSF". So their
condition 1 is the canonical QSF, which `qsf_identity.py` measures using the
producer's own `canonical_qsf_sha256` — `BUILD_ID` zeroed, seed and per-run
project path dropped, checkout prefix rewritten. All four are byte-identical,
recipes included, at no Quartus cost at all
(`reports-scope/zx81-quartus-qsf.log`). Their rows under `reports/` and
`reports-pass2/` were produced by `build_fes_zx81_oss` and now describe the
advisory nextpnr lane, not the shipping package.

**#1881 removed the 130 MHz RAM Tester package** and rewrote
`build_fes_ramtest.py`. The `--gpu-router` option was re-applied on main's
shape; the 130 MHz target, its timing diagnostic and the `memory_mhz` argument
stay deleted. `cores/fes-ramtest/rtl/ram_font.v` also moved, so `fes.ramtest`
is the one affected artefact whose *compiled* source set changed across the
base move and whose condition 1 could not simply carry forward.

**#1863 merged `check_level`.** Its rule drops conditions 2 and 3 for a core
recorded `reduced`, but puts that core back under the full rule for a change
touching "the core's own RTL, programs, toolchain lock or placement recipe".
This PR edits producers, which hold both the Yosys program and the nextpnr
options, so that clause is discharged by measurement, not by assertion — see
below. Eleven affected artefacts are `full` and four are `reduced`
(`fes.pong`, `fes.ramtest`, `fes.coleco`, `fes.riscv`).

**The third move (#1951, #1940, #1954, #1917, #1964) moved nothing of this
PR's.** `design_identity.py` and `route_inputs.py` between the two merge bases
report all fifteen affected artefacts' design identities and every
route-deciding input byte-identical (`reports-scope3/base-move3-design.log`,
`reports-scope3/route-inputs-base-move3.log`). So D12's merge-forward clause
carries conditions 1 and 2 across it on its first limb -- compiled source set
and route-deciding inputs both unchanged -- with no netlist run and no GPU time.
The one row that did move, `fes.pcxt-dos`, is main's own RTL change and is not
in this PR's scope.

**The fourth move (#1978, #1959, #1977, #1971, #1946, #1965, #1963, #1969)
moved nothing of this PR's either,** and was merged because #1978 is what
clears the CI failure described under "CI" below. The same two programs between
`5e592f003` and `6ee9f1113` report all fifteen affected artefacts' design
identities and every route-deciding input byte-identical
(`reports-scope4/base-move4-design.log`,
`reports-scope4/route-inputs-base-move4.log`), so the merge-forward clause
carries conditions 1 and 2 across this move on its first limb as well, again
with no netlist run and no GPU time.

Unlike the third move, no *whole-closure* digest moved either, which is read
from the measurement rather than inferred from which files the diff touches:
`closure_sha256` is identical across the move for 19 of the 20 rows that have
one in `reports-scope4/base-move4-design.json`, the exception being
`fes.pcxt-dos` (`fes.pc386` has none, for the reason given above).

`fes.pcxt-dos` is again the single moved row, this time through
`scripts/yosys_netlist.py`, which #1959 edited. That file is in no other
affected artefact's closure, and the closure digests are what say so: a shared
script inside another row's closure could not have moved while that row's
`closure_sha256` stayed identical. So the row is out of scope for the same
reason as before, and not merely by the support matrix's say-so.

One thing in that result looks alarming and is not: *every* row's whole-closure
digest moved, including the fourteen whose design identity did not. #1951 edited
`scripts/input_identity.py`, which sits in every producer's closure but is
`packaging` role, so a design-v1 `BUILD_ID` is not taken over it.
`packaging_role_proof.py` makes the code say that instead of me: in each tree it
builds a format-2 record, pushes it through the real identity path
(`with_identities` then `functional_key`, which is what `build_identity`
reduces a format-2 record to), and perturbs one closure entry at a time. The
packaging-role entry does not move the identity; a design-role entry does. The
second half is the point -- a checker that was differencing nothing at all would
pass the first half too (`reports-scope3/packaging-role.log`).

**#1951 also registered a sixteenth artefact, `fes.pc386`**, whose producer is
the first to live outside `scripts/`. That broke `design_identity.py`, which
derived the module from the file's stem: the new row raised
`ModuleNotFoundError`, and because the traceback stopped the loop, it also hid

The loader is now path-based. For `fes.pc386` itself the program refuses by

`fes.pc386` is not an affected artefact and owes nothing here
(`reports-scope3/pc386-scope.log` at the third base, 158 paths;
`reports-scope4/pc386-scope.log` at the fourth, 161).

That count is the program's own drift signal, and it moved, so it was
rechecked rather than noted: the three added paths are
`cores/fes-common/rtl/i386/fes_x87_trans{,_pkg,_rom}.sv`, which #1959 added to

## The check_level clause, discharged mechanically

`front_end_program.py` compares the exact command lists each producer's
`build_commands` emits, `BUILD_ID` pinned to 32 zeros and one identical set of
tool paths on both sides. All thirteen nextpnr cases are byte-identical
(`reports-scope/front-end-programs.log`).

That alone does **not** cover the placement recipe, because `fes.spectrum`,
`fes.ramtest`, `fes.c64` and `fes.coleco` ignore the route tuple

reads the toolchain lock, QSF, SDC and both `pnr_recipe` digests, taking each
path from the producer's own declared input list rather than a guessed
attribute name. Every one is byte-identical across this change
(`reports-scope/route-inputs.log`).

### How condition 2 is measured, and the control that makes it mean anything

The producers derive `BUILD_ID` from their own build record and offer no
override, and all four of the remaining cores put it in the netlist with
`chparam`. So the two sides' natural seals necessarily differ: a different
constant gives different RBF bytes. That difference is not condition 2.
Condition 2 is whether anything *else* changed the hardware.

`rbf_at_build_id.py` measures it by holding the `BUILD_ID` **equal** on both
sides and comparing the RBFs. The route is a function of the netlist, device,
QSF, SDC, frequency, seed list, weights, recipe and nextpnr binary; this PR
changes none of those but the netlist, whose only difference is that constant.
The tool calls each producer's own `build_commands` and its own route function
rather than reimplementing the build — three of the four route against a QSF
generated during the build, so a replica would have to reproduce producer
internals, and one that drifted would read as valid evidence while being none.

**The tool is checked against the producer before it is trusted.** Run on the
main tree at main's own `BUILD_ID`, it must reproduce main's normal-seal RBF
byte for byte. For `fes.menu` it does:

| run | BUILD_ID | RBF sha256 | bytes |

| main, producer, normal seal | `60bccd54…` | `6107b13e0425af1b…` | 2,172,530 |
| main, tool, forced to the same id | `60bccd54…` | `6107b13e0425af1b…` | 2,172,530 |

## Method

`netlist_digest.py` runs a producer's own Yosys command and nothing else: no
placement, no routing, no GPU. Two properties make the comparison exact rather
than approximate:

- **The `BUILD_ID` is pinned to 32 zeros on both sides**, as the producers that
  already have `--synth-only` do. The constant that D12 exempts is therefore
  identical in both netlists instead of being masked out afterwards, and any
  surviving difference is the source change itself.
- **The Yosys program uses only relative paths** (`read_verilog -sv -I
  cores/<core>/generated <relative sources>`, `write_json <relative>`), run with
  the tree as the working directory. The `src` attributes are relative, so the
  second exemption D12 allows does not arise either: the two trees' netlists are
  directly comparable, with nothing normalised away.

Both sides run against **one** cache root, so one tool install produces both
netlists, as D12 requires.

### The two sides are in different checkout paths

D12 says to run both sides "in the same checkout path where that is possible",
and here it was not: main is a separate worktree, so the two trees necessarily
sit at different absolute paths. That is worth stating plainly rather than
leaving implicit, because a sibling session measuring #1806 found `fes.spectrum`
differing across two checkouts of identical content — 14,593 cells against
13,532, and 15% different fmax — while being byte-identical within either path.

For **this** record the extra variable only strengthens the result: every one of
the fourteen comparisons **matched** across two different paths, so each is a
match despite a variable D12 would have let us remove, not because of it. A
mismatch is what would have needed re-running in one path before anything could
be concluded, and none occurred.

`fes.spectrum` is a direct counter-observation to a path effect on that same
core: measured here across two paths, with one Yosys binary, its netlists are
byte-identical (`3f2dfb76…`). That does not refute the sibling's measurement —
different Yosys build, different flow — but it does mean a path effect is not
reproducible for that core under this one.

### Two presentations of DeanoC/yosys#77, and why each core is measured twice

`3a5accbd` is intermittently unreliable on some cores, in two distinct ways
that the sibling session measuring #1806 established by auditing six runs of
one core:

- about **1 run in 5** segfaults in `OPT_CLEAN`;
- about **1 run in 6** exits **0** and writes a materially different netlist —
  in the audited case 1,061 cells smaller with 15% different fmax, from a run
  that was not short of memory or time.

`netlist_digest.py` deletes the netlist before synthesising and refuses to
record a run that did not write one, so the crashing presentation shows up as a
failed run rather than a stale file silently compared. **That guard does not
catch the silent presentation**, which exits 0 with a plausible file.

Only repetition catches that one. A single measurement per side is not enough:
if both sides happened to hit the same bad variant, the comparison would read
as a match on an artefact rather than on the cores' actual netlists. So **every
core is measured twice per side**, in two independent passes, and
`summarise.py` requires the two passes to agree before it will call a core
byte-identical. Agreement across passes also makes each side's netlist
demonstrably reproducible, which is the property the silent presentation would
break.

```
python3 netlist_digest.py --tree <tree>/sources/misteross --core fes.pong \
    --cache-root /data/fes1861-netlist/cache --out reports/<core>-<side>
```

## A margin that moves: `fes.spectrum-plus3` between 1.01x and 1.11x

Recorded here rather than left in the table, because the shipping number alone
would misdescribe this core. **The seal that ships closes by 11%** (`516d9107`,
below). One identity earlier it closed by 1%, with identical RTL. The margin is
a property of the identity, not of the design, so neither number on its own is
the truth about it.

The first measured identity, `678c01a1`:

| clock | achieved | constraint | ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| audio | 191.095 MHz | 12.288 MHz | 15.5513x |
| pixel | 92.379 MHz | 74.250 MHz | 1.2442x |
| system | **52.768 MHz** | **52.225 MHz** | **1.0104x** |

Both seals pass on their own gates and nothing was tuned, waived or re-run to
make either pass. The point is what happens next. A core that clears by one per
cent is one whose next `BUILD_ID` move may miss, and D12 exists precisely
because that constant is synthesised in and reshuffles placement. When such a
miss happens it will arrive in whatever PR is in flight at the time and will
read as that PR's regression. It is not.

This same PR already demonstrates the effect on this same core, which is why
the claim is not speculative. At main's `BUILD_ID` 9ac4506e, placer seed 5
could not route plus3 at all: it stalled at `overused=1` with `tmgfail=0`
across about 1300 router iterations, on both legs independently, and seed 2 was
the one that closed. At this PR's own `BUILD_ID` 678c01a1, seed 5 routes it on
the first attempt. Nothing changed but the constant.

The comparable case is `fes.ramtest` in the #1881 record, whose three measured
identities gave 102.375, 101.440 and 100.980 MHz against a 100.0 MHz
constraint with no functional change at all.

### A second identity, measured, and why it does not retire the warning

The second main move forced this core to be resealed, which gave a second
measurement of the same RTL at a different identity one merge apart:

| identity | winning seed | system clock | ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `678c01a1` | 5 | 52.768 MHz | 1.0104x |
| `516d9107` | 4 | **57.727 MHz** | **1.1054x** |

Nothing functional differs between those two builds. The achieved system
frequency moved 9.4%, the binding margin went from 1% to 11%, and the seed
that wins changed. The current seal is comfortable.

That is **not** a reason to drop the warning; it is the evidence for it. One
identity of the two sat 1% above constraint, so the distribution for this core
includes values that close by a whisker, and nothing says 1.0104x was the
lowest it can go. Reporting only the comfortable second measurement would be
choosing the flattering half of a two-point sample. The honest statement is
that `fes.spectrum-plus3`'s margin is identity-dependent across at least
1.01x-1.11x, and a future `BUILD_ID` can therefore miss on a PR that changed
nothing about this core.

`fes.ramtest` (#1926) and `fes.coleco` (#1894) are already tracked as thin; on
this evidence `fes.spectrum-plus3` belongs in that set, and that is a follow-up
rather than anything this PR should change.

The table is rendered into those markers by `python3 summarise.py --write`, so
no digest in it is transcribed by hand.

**All fourteen affected cores produce byte-identical netlists.** That covers
both kinds of change: the four whose own producer script this PR edits
(`fes.pong`, `fes.menu`, `fes.ramtest`, `fes.riscv`) and the ten that move only
through the shared helper. `fes.coleco` is the only one on a different Yosys pin
(`5391eeb1` rather than `3a5accbd`), so it exercised its own toolchain rather
than the shared one.

The `Pinned inputs` column is the argument in one line: those digests **differ**
on every row, because the edited producer scripts and the shared helper are
themselves pinned inputs and are exactly why `BUILD_ID` moves — and the netlist
is identical anyway.

`fes.pong` is the strongest case in the set: both its own producer script and
the shared helper changed. `cmp` reports the two 3,294,796-byte netlists
identical, over a top module of 1,422 cells and 1,804 nets, from one Yosys
binary. Nothing was normalised or excluded. `fes.menu` is the same kind of case
through the package producer, and the ZX81 models stand for the ten cores whose
closure picks up only the shared helper.

### `fes.splash` is the one artifact that needs D12's `src` exemption

Splash's **raw** digests differ between the two sides, and the table says so
rather than hiding it. The difference is entirely in `src` attributes naming
the Yosys install directory: splash refuses a shared cache root and
authenticates a local install at `<tree>/build/toolchain`, so the two sides
necessarily use two installs at different paths, and Yosys records that path in
the `src` of cells it reads from its own `share/yosys` models.

D12 exempts exactly that — "file paths in `src` attributes (the checkout and
the tool install directory: a toolchain at the same commit is the same
toolchain wherever it is installed)". The two installs are byte-identical
binaries of the same commit: the main side's is a copy of the branch side's,
both `yosys` at sha256 `d1d8c98fac8b1155f50ef029…`.

`netlist_digest.py` records `netlist_sha256_src_exempt` for every run, which
replaces that run's own tree prefix **and nothing else**, so any difference
that is not a tree path survives into it. Splash's exempted digests agree on
both sides in both passes (`68858653e28f06b8…`). The other 14 artifacts share
one install through the cache root and match byte for byte without the
exemption, so for them the two digests are the same number.

Each lock needed its own nextpnr built before its producer would authenticate,
even for a Yosys-only run. Because the shared cache is content-addressed per
tool *and* commit, one cache root served every lock: after the first, each
additional lock cost a single nextpnr build rather than a full toolchain.
